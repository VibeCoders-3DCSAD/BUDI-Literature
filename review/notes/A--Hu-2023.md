## Summary

A new framework, Two-Stage Predict+Optimize, for constrained optimization problems where some parameters are unknown at solving time and must be predicted from features. The framework treats the estimated solution as a soft commitment that can be modified once true parameters are revealed, formulating the correction itself as a Stage 2 optimization problem that includes a penalty function for changing the Stage 1 solution. The paper provides a general end-to-end training algorithm applicable to all mixed integer linear programs (MILPs), adapting interior-point differentiation through KKT conditions to compute gradients of a surrogate loss. Experiments on alloy production, 0-1 knapsack, and nurse scheduling benchmarks demonstrate superior post-hoc regret compared with classical regression methods, the prior IntOpt-C method, and CombOptNet.

## Problem and Motivation

Optimization problems often have parameters that are unknown at solving time but can be predicted from relevant features using historical data. Prior work in Predict+Optimize focused almost exclusively on unknowns appearing in the optimization objective, not the constraints. When unknowns appear in constraints, the estimated solution may be infeasible under true parameters. Hu et al. [12] proposed the first adaptation for constrained unknowns, requiring a correction function to amend infeasible solutions, but this framework has shortcomings: the correction function is ad-hoc, restricts applicability to packing and covering LPs, and cannot handle cases where correction is desirable even when the estimated solution is feasible. The paper aims to provide a simpler, more general framework for all MILPs.

## Method

**Design.** The paper states no formal design label; it is a framework proposal with algorithmic development and comparative benchmark experiments against classical and state-of-the-art methods.
**Sample.** n = 3 benchmark problem families (brass alloy production, titanium-alloy production, 0-1 knapsack, nurse scheduling); unit of analysis = individual optimization instance. Training/test splits: 350/150 instances for each alloy, 700/300 for 0-1 knapsack, 210/90 for NSP.
**Context.** geography: Not reported (public datasets: Kaggle credit-risk dataset by Paulus et al., NSPLib, ICON scheduling competition); population: Not applicable (optimization instances, not human participants); setting: Computational — offline experiments using Gurobi for MILP solving, PyTorch for neural networks, scikit-learn for classical regression.

The Two-Stage Predict+Optimize framework operates as follows. In Stage 1, unknown parameters are estimated from features and the Stage 1 optimization problem is solved using estimated parameters, yielding a soft commitment solution. In Stage 2, true parameters are revealed, and a new optimization problem is solved that augments the original problem with a penalty function for changing the Stage 1 solution. The final solution is evaluated by post-hoc regret, which compares the objective under true parameters plus the penalty against the true optimal value.

For training, the paper adapts the interior-point approach of Mandi and Guns to compute gradients through the KKT conditions of a convex relaxation of the MILP. The surrogate loss replaces the true MILP solutions in both stages with solutions from log-barrier relaxed problems at a fixed barrier weight. Gradient calculations for all problem parameters (c, A, b, G, h) are derived via implicit differentiation of KKT conditions, using the implicit function theorem. The training algorithm then combines these gradients with standard backpropagation.

## Key Findings

- The Two-Stage Predict+Optimize framework always achieves smaller mean post-hoc regret than the Hu et al. framework on the alloy production problem (Table 2), with 6.18%-35.63% smaller regret in brass production and 6.59%-29.89% smaller regret in titanium-alloy production.
- The proposed 2S training method achieves the best performance compared with IntOpt-C and classical regression methods on alloy production (Table 3), with 1.20%-3.18% smaller mean post-hoc regrets than IntOpt-C in brass production and 1.18%-5.33% smaller in titanium-alloy production.
- On the 0-1 knapsack problem (Table 4), 2S has significantly better results than CombOptNet, Ridge, k-NN, CART, RF, and NN; the advantage becomes more significant as knapsack capacity increases.
- On the nurse scheduling problem (Table 5), 2S obtains at least 7.61%, 15.65%, 17.99%, 20.51%, 46.76%, and 62.49% smaller post-hoc regret than other classical methods at penalty factors 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0, and 8.0 respectively.
- As penalty magnitude increases, Stage 1 solutions become more likely to remain feasible under true parameters, with feasibility rising from 0.00% at penalty 0.05 to 99.07% at penalty 4 (Table 6).
- The 2S method is 30%-50% faster than CvxpyLayer with comparable or better post-hoc regret (Tables 10-11).
- The l2 projection framework performs worse than both the Two-Stage framework and the Hu et al. framework on alloy production (Table 12).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The authors acknowledge that both stages must be expressible as MILPs, constraining optimization objectives to be linear in the MILP decision variables (Section 4, p. 7). This contrasts with the Hu et al. framework which handles non-linear penalties.
- The authors acknowledge that MILPs can handle some non-linearity by using extra decision variables, and that gradient calculations can be adapted to handle general differentiable non-linear objectives (Section 4, p. 7).
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not report confidence intervals or p-values for any of the main comparative results; only means and standard deviations across 10 runs are given.
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not report per-instance breakdowns, variance decomposition, or effect sizes for the post-hoc regret improvements.
- [unacknowledged] The nurse scheduling problem uses data from a different problem (ICON scheduling competition) as numerical values for patient load demands and features, rather than real NSP data, which limits external validity.
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not report training time for the 2S method on the NSP separately from test time, though Table 14 reports 537.32 seconds.
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not discuss potential negative societal impacts or ethical considerations of deploying the framework in real optimization settings.

## Definitions

- **MILP** — Mixed Integer Linear Program: an optimization problem with linear objective and constraints, where some variables are constrained to be integers.
- **Para-OP** — Parameterized optimization problem: an optimization problem where objective and/or constraints depend on a vector of parameters θ.
- **Two-Stage Predict+Optimize** — The proposed framework where Stage 1 solves the optimization with estimated parameters, and Stage 2 solves a new optimization with true parameters plus a penalty for deviating from the Stage 1 solution.
- **Post-hoc regret** — The sum of (a) the difference in objective between the true optimal solution and the Stage 2 solution under true parameters, and (b) the penalty incurred from correction.
- **Soft commitment** — The Stage 1 solution, which can be modified in Stage 2 at a cost/penalty.
- **Penalty function** — A function Pen(x*₁ → x) capturing the cost of changing from Stage 1 solution x*₁ to a Stage 2 solution x.
- **Surrogate loss** — A differentiable approximation of the post-hoc regret used for training, constructed by replacing MILP solutions with solutions from log-barrier relaxed problems.
- **IntOpt-C** — The prior state-of-the-art Predict+Optimize training method for packing and covering LPs with unknown parameters in constraints, by Hu et al. [12].
- **CombOptNet** — A method for learning integer programming constraints that focuses on making predicted optimal solutions close to true optimal solutions in solution space, with a different goal and loss function.

## Key Equations

- Post-hoc regret: `P Reg(θ̂, θ) = obj(x*₂, θ) + P en(x*₁ → x*₂, θ) − obj(x*(θ), θ)`
- Stage 1 OP: `x*₁ = arg min_x obj(x, θ̂) s.t. C(x, θ̂)`
- Stage 2 OP: `x*₂ = arg min_x obj(x, θ) + P en(x*₁ → x, θ) s.t. C(x, θ)`
- MILP standard form: `x* = arg min cᵀx s.t. Ax = b, Gx ≥ h, x ≥ 0, x_S ∈ Z`
- Interior-point relaxation: `x* = arg min cᵀx − µ Σ ln(x_i) − µ Σ ln(s_i) s.t. Ax = b, Gx − s = h`
- Gradient chain rule: `dP Reg(θ̂, θ)/dw_e = (∂P Reg/∂x*₂)|_{x*₁} · (∂x*₂/∂x*₁) · (∂x*₁/∂θ̂) · (∂θ̂/∂w_e) + (∂P Reg/∂x*₁)|_{x*₂} · (∂x*₁/∂θ̂) · (∂θ̂/∂w_e)`

## Remember This

- The Two-Stage Predict+Optimize framework treats the Stage 1 solution as a soft commitment and formulates correction as a Stage 2 optimization with a penalty function.
- The framework applies to all MILPs and requires only a penalty function, not a correction function.
- Training uses a surrogate loss based on interior-point LP relaxation and KKT-condition gradients, adapted from Mandi and Guns.
- The 2S method achieves the best post-hoc regret across alloy production, 0-1 knapsack, and nurse scheduling benchmarks.
- Feasibility of Stage 1 solutions under true parameters increases from 0.00% at penalty 0.05 to 99.07% at penalty 4.
