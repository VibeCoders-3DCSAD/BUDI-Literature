# A Constrained, Data-Driven Budgeting Framework Integrating Macro Demand Forecasting and Marketing Response Modeling

`A--Lu-2025` — Lu (2025), *Journal of Technology Informatics and Engineering, 4(3), 493-520* \[10.51903/jtie.v4i3.466]

## Summary

An end-to-end budgeting framework forecasts category demand from public PCE data, learns diminishing-returns marketing response curves, and allocates spend under SG&A and cash-flow guardrails calibrated from a Form 10-K, showing that caps met under point forecasts breach constraints under Monte Carlo demand uncertainty.

## Problem and Motivation

Budgeting is one of the most repeated and consequential management processes inside organizations, and a budget must be simultaneously a forecast, a commitment device and a control system (Sec. I, p. 493). Budget inputs arrive from heterogeneous sources at different temporal resolutions — macro indicators monthly or quarterly, internal sales daily, ratio-based constraints quarterly or annually — and a coherent pipeline must combine them without losing interpretability (Sec. I, pp. 493-494). Marketing spend is the hard case, because its benefits are uncertain, nonlinear and often delayed, so linear response models produce unrealistic implied returns at high budgets, while accounting constraints can still make a positive-expected-return investment unacceptable (Sec. I, pp. 494-495).

## Method

**Design.** Computational method-development study with a rolling-origin backtest, cross-validated response-model comparison, sensitivity analyses and Monte Carlo evaluation of a constrained allocation problem; the authors state no formal design label.
**Sample.** n = 63 quarterly observations per series for each of three PCE components (2010-01-01 to 2025-07-01), 200 cross-sectional advertising observations, and one annual filing (Apple Inc. FY2025); unit of analysis is a quarterly time-series point for forecasting and a candidate portfolio for allocation
**Context.** geography: United States national accounts (FRED/BEA) and a US SEC registrant (Apple Inc.); population: Not applicable — economic aggregate series and a firm-level filing, no human participants; setting: Virtual/computational: offline analysis of public data, described as a pedagogical reference implementation with no live firm deployment (Sec. I, p. 495).

- Instantiate three public data sources covering the pipeline: FRED PCE components PCDG, PCND and PCESV as a macro demand proxy; the ISLR Advertising.csv for response estimation; Apple Inc.'s FY2025 Form 10-K for constraint calibration (Sec. III.A, Table 1, p. 497).
- Restrict the macro series to 2010Q1-2025Q3 to include the COVID-19 structural break, whose categories behave differently: durables spike, services decline sharply, nondurables stay relatively stable (Sec. III.A, p. 498).
- Evaluate four demand forecasting classes — seasonal naive, SARIMAX(1,1,1)x(0,1,1,4), gradient boosting on lags 1-4, and VAR on log differences with AIC-selected lag up to 4 — re-estimated at every rolling origin (Sec. III.C, Table 3, p. 500).
- Score forecasts with RMSE and MAPE under a rolling one-step-ahead backtest over 2018Q1-2025Q3 with an expanding training window from 2010Q1, giving 31 forecast evaluations per series (Sec. III.C and Sec. IV, pp. 501-502, 508).
- Fit five marketing response models — OLS, ridge, lasso, gradient boosting and a Hill saturation function estimated by bounded nonlinear least squares — and compare them by 5-fold cross-validation because the Advertising data are cross-sectional with no temporal ordering (Sec. III.C, Table 4, pp. 502-503).
- Compute gross margin, SG&A, R&D and operating-cash-flow ratios from the 10-K, then set the marketing budget cap at 1.5% of revenue so non-marketing SG&A is 5.13%, matching the audited 6.63% total (Sec. III.C, Tables 6, p. 505 and p. 506).
- Link macro demand to firm revenue by a constant market-share parameter calibrated on the most recent observation, and scale response-model units into dollars by calibrating a 1% marketing spend to a 3% revenue uplift (Sec. III.C, Eqs. 6-7, p. 505; p. 513).
- Impose an SG&A budget constraint and a liquidity constraint with a minimum cash-flow ratio of 0.25, then derive a chance constraint showing that spending at the deterministic cap implies a violation probability of P(e < 1) (Sec. III.C, Eqs. 9-13, pp. 506-507).
- Search allocations by grid search on 5% increments from 20% to 100% of the cap, giving 3,927 candidate portfolios per scenario, each evaluated with 500 Monte Carlo scenarios that bootstrap multiplicative error ratios from the VAR aggregate backtest (Sec. III.C, p. 507; Sec. III.C, p. 508).
- Sweep three macro scenarios (−5%, 0%, +5%) and a risk-aversion parameter λ in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}, then vary the cap ratio κ and the effectiveness normalization φ as sensitivity analyses (Sec. III.C, p. 508; Tables 12-13, pp. 515-516).

## Key Findings

- num: Abstract, p. 493 reports that multivariate models improve total-demand accuracy to approximately 2.85% MAPE; Table 8, p. 509 prints aggregate MAPE values of 6.06, 2.92, 2.86 and 2.85 across the four model rows.
- num: Table 7, p. 508 gives PCND the lowest errors of the three series (SARIMAX RMSE 83.27, MAPE 1.65) while the seasonal naive row for the same series is the worst of the four (RMSE 229.49, MAPE 5.22).
- num: Table 4, p. 503 shows gradient boosting the best response-model fit (CV_RMSE 0.661, CV R2 0.984) and OLS, ridge and lasso tied at CV_RMSE 1.722 and CV R2 0.891; the Hill model sits between at 1.449 and 0.922.
- num: Table 5, p. 503 fits a newspaper coefficient of 0.0 against 37.95 for TV and 31.7322 for radio, and marginal-response plots confirm negligible incremental newspaper sales over the observed range (Sec. IV, p. 510).
- num: Table 9, p. 513 reports a baseline optimum of 1,016.25 ($m) total spend, 0.65 utilization of a 1,563.47 ($m) cap, TV share 254.06 and radio share 762.19 with newspaper at 0.0.
- num: The same optimum implies incremental revenue of 5,735.5 ($m), an incremental revenue-to-spend ratio of roughly 5.6×, and mean operating profit of 35,961.44 ($m) with standard deviation 2,131.3 ($m) (Table 9, p. 513).
- num: Sec. III.C, p. 507 finds the empirical revenue error distribution gives P(e < 1) close to 40%, so spending at the deterministic ratio cap would breach the constraint in roughly 40% of realizations.
- num: Table 10, p. 513 shows the channel shares fixed at TV 25.0 / radio 75.0 / newspaper 0.0 across −5%, 0% and +5% revenue scenarios, with optimal spend scaling 965.44 to 1,067.07 ($m).
- num: Table 11, p. 513 shows λ ≥ 1.0 cutting utilization to 0.60 and spend to 938.08 ($m) with mean profit falling only to 35,958.83 ($m) and standard profit to 2,126.64 ($m).
- num: Table 12, p. 515 shows tightening the cap to 1.0% lowering the SG&A satisfaction rate to 0.948 with SG&A satisfaction of 1.0 at 1.5% and 2.0%, and Table 13, p. 516 shows φ = 0.05 driving satisfaction to 0.598 against 1.0 at φ = 0.02 and 0.03.
- num: Sec. IV, p. 509 reports that nondurables show the lowest MAPE and that budgeting buffers should be larger in categories whose demand is more discretionary or more sensitive to shocks.

## Limitations and Gaps

- The authors name their main limitation first: the data sources are not internally consistent, because the macro PCE series represent the whole economy while Advertising.csv is a small cross-sectional toy problem, so the normalization used to map sales units to revenue dollars "should not be interpreted as an estimate of true marketing ROI for any specific firm" (Sec. V.A, pp. 516-517).
- Marketing response is treated as contemporaneous; the authors note real marketing effects often persist and that dynamic response modeling would turn the problem into a multi-period allocation problem (Sec. V.A, p. 517).
- The results narrative contradicts its own accuracy table: Sec. IV, p. 508 states "the seasonal naïve baseline is difficult to beat for the smoother nondurable series," while Table 7, p. 508 makes the seasonal naive row the worst of all four models on both metrics for every series, including nondurables at RMSE 229.49 and MAPE 5.22.
- The constraint narrative is also internally inconsistent: Sec. III.C, p. 506 says the calibrated values imply "the SG&A constraint is binding in the present setting," while Table 9, p. 513 reports utilization of 0.65 and Sec. IV, p. 510 states the optimizer "selects a spend level below the cap." Both readings cannot hold for the reported solution.
- Table 9's projected revenue of 109,966.73 ($m) disagrees with the 104,231.24 ($m) baseline revenue forecast in Table 10, p. 513; the reported cap of 1,563.47 ($m) is consistent with 1.5% of 104,231.24, not of 109,966.73.
- Table 8, p. 509 prints four aggregate rows without model row labels in the conversion, so which RMSE pairs with which MAPE cannot be reconstructed from the table alone; the abstract's "≈2.85% MAPE" and the text's attribution of the aggregate gain to VAR identify only the best row.
- The authors use latest-vintage FRED data and state this "can overstate performance compared with a strict real-time evaluation," and they abstract away fiscal-versus-calendar quarter alignment (Sec. III.B, p. 499).
- [unacknowledged] The macro module works at quarterly frequency on 63 observations with a 32-observation pre-test training window, so nothing here tests a monthly or individual-level series.
- [unacknowledged] The paper reports no confidence interval, no p-value and no per-fold or per-scenario dispersion for any accuracy or profit figure; RMSE, MAPE, CV metrics and Monte Carlo means are printed as point values only.
- [unacknowledged] Marketing response is estimated from observational cross-sectional data the authors describe as a "stylized approximation designed to highlight the mechanics of optimization rather than to provide causal estimates," so the marginal ROI curves are not causally identified (Sec. III.B, p. 499).
- [unacknowledged] Table 8 aside, the comparison is single-sample and single-firm: one macro proxy, one textbook response dataset and one registrant, so the framework's own numerical results carry no external validity.

## Definitions

- **FP&A** — financial planning and analysis; the organisational budgeting process used as the paper's case study (Sec. I, p. 493).
- **PCE components** — Personal Consumption Expenditures for durable goods (PCDG), nondurable goods (PCND) and services (PCESV), seasonally adjusted annual rates in billions of dollars, used as the macro demand proxy (Sec. III.A, p. 498).
- **SAAR** — seasonally adjusted annual rate; dividing a quarterly SAAR value by four gives the corresponding quarterly level (Sec. III.A, p. 498).
- **Hill saturation function** — the concave response term h(.) used to model diminishing marginal returns in each marketing channel, fitted by bounded nonlinear least squares (Sec. III.C, Eq. 5, p. 503).
- **Marketing cap (κ)** — the allowable ratio of marketing spend to revenue, set to 1.5% of revenue from the audited SG&A split (Sec. III.C, p. 506).
- **Effectiveness normalization (φ)** — the assumed baseline revenue lift produced by a 1% marketing spend, calibrated so the baseline is 3% (Sec. III.C, pp. 506-507).
- **Chance constraint** — P(B ≤ κR) ≥ 1 − δ, bounding the probability that realized revenue breaches a ratio-based cap (Sec. III.C, Eq. 12, p. 507).
- **Risk-aversion parameter (λ)** — the weight in the mean–risk objective, interpretable as dollars of expected profit sacrificed to reduce profit volatility by one dollar (Sec. III.C, p. 508).
- **Constraint satisfaction rate** — the Monte Carlo proportion of simulated scenarios in which an SG&A or cash-flow constraint holds; the paper's governance deliverable (Sec. I, p. 495).
- **Budget utilization (util)** — the selected spend as a fraction of the cap; 0.65 at the baseline solution (Table 9, p. 513).

## Key Equations

- `P_t = Σ_k y_{k,t}` — aggregate demand as the sum of the category-level demands.
- `RMSE = sqrt((1/n) Σ_t (y_t − ŷ_t)²)` — root mean squared error; penalises large errors more heavily.
- `MAPE = sqrt((1/n) Σ_t |(y_t − ŷ_t)/y_t|)` — mean absolute percentage error, scale-free and interpretable as a percentage.
- `ŷ_t = y_{t−4}` — seasonal naive forecast projecting from the corresponding quarter of the previous year.
- `Sales(s) = b0 + Σ_i b_i h(s_i; α_i, γ_i)` — Hill saturation response with per-channel saturation parameters.
- `ρ = Revenue_annual / P_last` — constant market-share parameter linking macro demand to firm revenue.
- `R̂_{t+1} = ρ P̂_{t+1} / 4` — quarterly firm revenue approximated from the macro forecast.
- `Π(b) = (g − o)(R + ΔR(b)) − Σ b_i` — operating profit objective; g − o ≈ 0.335 after non-marketing operating expenses.
- `Σ b_i ≤ 0.015 R` — SG&A budget constraint capping total marketing expenditure.
- `Σ b_i ≤ (c_FO − c_min) R` — liquidity constraint with minimum cash-flow ratio c_min = 0.25.
- `B ≤ κ R̂ q_δ` — chance constraint under a multiplicative revenue error model R = e R̂.
- `ℙ(e < 1)` — the violation probability implied by spending at the deterministic cap; close to 40% empirically.

## Remember This

- Forecast, response and constraint modules feed one grid-searched budget allocation with Monte Carlo satisfaction rates.
- SARIMAX(1,1,1)x(0,1,1,4) is benchmarked against seasonal naive, gradient boosting and VAR on 63 quarterly PCE observations.
- A cap met under a point forecast breaches the constraint in roughly 40% of simulated realizations.
- Baseline optimum spends 0.97% of revenue, 25% TV and 75% radio, with both satisfaction rates at 1.0.
- The authors state data inconsistency as the main limitation, and the results text contradicts Table 7 on the seasonal naive baseline.
