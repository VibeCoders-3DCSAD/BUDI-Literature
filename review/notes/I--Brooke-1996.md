`I--Brooke-1996` — Brooke (1996), in P. W. Jordan et al. (eds.), *Usability Evaluation in Industry*, Taylor & Francis, London, 189–194.

## Summary

John Brooke introduces the System Usability Scale (SUS), a 10-item Likert-scale questionnaire designed for quick, low-cost global assessments of system usability. SUS produces a single composite score from 0-100 and is intended to be used across a wide range of contexts.

## Problem and Motivation

There is a need for broad general measures of usability that can be used to compare systems across contexts, as well as quick and dirty methods for low-cost usability assessments in industrial settings.

## Method

**Design.** Instrument development / methodological description.  
**Sample.** Not reported.  
**Context.** Geography: United Kingdom; population: Not reported; setting: industrial systems evaluation.

- Describe the need for context-independent yet comparable usability measures.
- Present the 10-item SUS questionnaire with alternating positive and negative items.
- Describe scoring: convert each item to 0-4, sum, multiply by 2.5 to yield 0-100.
- Advocate for its use as a quick global measure across contexts.

## Key Findings

- SUS is a reliable, low-cost usability scale that can be used for global assessments of system usability.
- The scale yields a single score (0-100) that allows comparison across systems and contexts.
- The method is intended to be quick to administer and score.

## Limitations and Gaps

- The original chapter does not present extensive psychometric validation data; later works (Lewis 2009, Bangor et al. 2008, Lewis & Sauro 2018) expand on reliability and interpretation.
