# Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2023

`I--FederalReserve-2024` — Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (2024), *Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System* \[Not reported]

## Summary

The 2023 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED) reports that near the end of 2023, 72% of U.S. adults were at least doing okay financially (39% "doing okay" plus 33% "living comfortably"). Sixty-three percent said they could cover a hypothetical $400 emergency expense exclusively with cash or its equivalent, and 68% could pay an expense of at least $500 from current savings — though 18% could handle only under $100 using savings alone. Sixty-five percent said changes in the prices they paid versus the prior year had made their financial situation worse.

## Problem and Motivation

The report measures the financial conditions, spending, and savings buffers of U.S. households, giving generalisable evidence on how fragile household finances are — the same fragility a personal financial management app must respond to.

## Method

**Design.** Annual online survey of U.S. adults (Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking, SHED), fielded in the fourth quarter (October 2023) of each year since 2013.
**Sample.** U.S. adults aged 18 and over; the survey is fielded online with a probability-based panel (more than 11,000 respondents in prior waves; the 2023 wave reports survey details in its appendices). Not applicable as an analytic sample in the academic-experimental sense.
**Context.** geography: United States; population: U.S. households/adults; setting: nationally representative panel survey.

- SHED uses a probability-based internet panel to be representative of the U.S. adult population, with supplemental measures to reach under-covered groups.
- Headline well-being is measured with the question "Overall, which one of the following best describes how well you are managing financially these days?"; "at least doing okay" = "doing okay" or "living comfortably".
- Emergency-expense cover is measured by whether adults could pay a hypothetical $400 expense exclusively using cash, savings, or a credit card paid off at the next statement (cash or its equivalent).

## Key Findings

- num: 72% of adults were at least doing okay financially (39% "doing okay" + 33% "living comfortably"); the rest reported difficulty (reported either "just getting by" or "finding it difficult to get by") (page 9).
- num: 63% of adults said they would cover a hypothetical $400 emergency expense exclusively using cash or its equivalent — unchanged from 2022 but down from a high of 68% in 2021 (page 6).
- num: 65% of adults said changes in the prices they paid compared with the prior year had made their financial situation worse, including 19% who said price changes had made their situation much worse (page 6).
- num: 18% of adults said the largest emergency expense they could handle right now using only savings was under $100; another 14% could handle $100 to $499 (page 37, Table 17).
- num: 68% of adults said they could pay an expense of at least $500 using only their current savings, unchanged from 2022 (page 37, Table 17).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The report is a U.S. institutional well-being survey; figures describe U.S. adults and should not be reported as Philippine household values. BUDGIE's local context is served by Philippine sources (PSA, BSP).
- SHED measures self-reported financial well-being and capability to handle stated hypothetical expenses, not actual balance-sheet savings buffers; statements about "thin buffers" supported here are behavioural (could/would cover an emergency), not ledger amounts.
- The $400 emergency-expense measure counts cash, savings, or a credit card paid off at the next statement as equivalent means; it is not a pure liquid-savings measure.

## Definitions

- **SHED (Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking)** — The Federal Reserve Board's annual survey of U.S. adults on economic well-being, fielded each fourth quarter since 2013.
- **At least doing okay financially** — Reporting either "doing okay" or "living comfortably" in answer to how well the household is managing financially these days.
- **Cash or its equivalent** — Completed payment for the emergency expense using cash or savings, or a credit card the adult pays off at the next statement.
