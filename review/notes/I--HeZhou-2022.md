# Household Financial Vulnerability to Income and Medical Expenditure Shocks: Measurement and Determinants

## Summary

The paper proposes an ex ante measure of household financial vulnerability: the probability that a household falls into financial distress when income and medical expenditure are uncertain. Using CHARLS data for 2011, 2013, and 2015, it finds that rural households are more vulnerable than urban households, that worse health and less education are associated with higher vulnerability, and that household size, labour force, education, disability, and health shocks contribute most to changes over time.

## Method

**Design.** Panel study with a probability-based ex ante vulnerability measure; the authors estimate income and medical-expenditure distributions, join them with a normal copula, and decompose the determinants of change.
**Sample.** Not reported in the main text; the study uses the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS) waves 2011, 2013, and 2015, with the household-year as the unit of analysis.

## Limitations and Gaps

- The measure depends on the estimated income and medical-expenditure distributions and on the normal copula; the paper does not report a formal limitations section.
