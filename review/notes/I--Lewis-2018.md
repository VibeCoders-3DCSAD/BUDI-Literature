## Summary

This article reviews the past, present, and future of the System Usability Scale. Lewis traces SUS origins to Brooke (1996), discusses its psychometric properties (reliability, validity), interpretation (adjectival ratings, percentile ranks), and common variations. The paper serves as a comprehensive reference for understanding and applying SUS.

## Problem and Motivation

SUS has become widely used but users often need guidance on interpretation, reliability, and best practices. The paper provides a retrospective and prospective view of SUS.

## Key Findings

- SUS remains a reliable and widely used global measure of perceived usability.
- Interpretation benefits from benchmarks, percentile ranks, and adjectival scales.
- The scale has evolved through empirical investigation since its introduction.
