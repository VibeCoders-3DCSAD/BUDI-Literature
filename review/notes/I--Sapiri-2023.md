# I--Sapiri-2023

## Summary

A correlational quantitative study testing whether financial attitude, financial behavior, financial knowledge and financial literacy predict the investment decision behavior of young investors aged 18-25 in Indonesia. Using multiple linear regression on 110 respondents, the study finds all four predictors have a positive and significant effect, both partially and simultaneously, on investment decision behavior, with financial attitude showing the strongest t-value (7.795) and financial literacy the weakest (2.196). The model reports an R of 0.990 and an R² of 0.980, and the authors conclude that young investors with positive attitudes, good financial behavior, and strong financial knowledge and literacy make better investment decisions.

## Problem and Motivation

Young investors have come to dominate the Indonesian capital market, with investors aged 18-25 numbering 375 thousand or 47.4% of new investors in 2021 (Sect. 1, p. 46). Despite this growth, many young investors make hasty decisions driven by emotion, follow others' investment choices, and rely on a single instrument because their financial planning knowledge is minimal (Sect. 1, p. 46). Financial literacy in Indonesia remains low at 38.03%, and in 2013 was reported at only 21.84% for the population over age 3 (Sect. 1, p. 47). The study aims to identify which financial attributes — attitude, behavior, knowledge, literacy — most influence young investors' decisions, so that issuers can better target this demographic and young investors can understand the determinants of their own decisions (Sect. 1, p. 48).

## Method

**Design.** Correlational quantitative descriptive study using multiple linear regression analysis; the authors label it "correlational research" with a "quantitative descriptive approach" (Sect. 3, p. 49).
**Sample.** n = 110 young investors aged 18-25 years, determined by the minimum sample method (5 × 22 indicators = 110); the population is 375 thousand or 47.4% of new investors in 2021 (Sect. 3, p. 49).
**Context.** geography: Indonesia (the paper cites Indonesian capital market data and the Indonesia Stock Exchange, but does not report a specific regional sampling frame); population: young investors aged 18-25 years; setting: survey data collected via questionnaire, analyzed with SPSS version 22.00 (Sect. 3, p. 49).

- Measure financial attitude with four items (X11-X14), financial behavior with four items (X21-X24), financial knowledge with three items (X31-X33), financial literacy with three items (X41-X43), and investment decision behavior with four items (Y11-Y14) (Table 1, p. 49).
- Validate items using corrected item-total correlation, comparing r-count to r-table (0.187 at df=108, α=0.05) (Sect. 4.1, p. 49).
- Test reliability with Cronbach's alpha, using a threshold greater than 0.60 (Sect. 4.1, p. 49).
- Test normality using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov Exact Sig. 2-tailed test, finding p = 0.178 > 0.05 (Sect. 4.2, p. 49).
- Test multicollinearity using tolerance (> 0.10) and VIF (< 10.00) (Sect. 4.2, p. 49).
- Test heteroscedasticity using the scatterplot method (Sect. 4.2, p. 49).
- Test autocorrelation using the Durbin-Watson method, reporting DW = 1.910 against dU = 1.785 and dL = 1.595 (Sect. 4.2, p. 50).
- Run multiple linear regression with financial attitude, financial behavior, financial knowledge and financial literacy as predictors of investment decision behavior (Sect. 4.3, p. 50).
- Compare t-values against t-table 1.983 and F-value against F-table 1.903 (Sect. 4.3, p. 50).

## Software

- SPSS version 22.00 (Sect. 3, p. 49)

## Key Findings

- Financial attitude has a positive and significant effect on investment decision behavior (t = 7.795, p = .000), the largest t-value among the four predictors (Table 2, p. 50; Sect. 4.3, p. 50).
- Financial behavior has a positive and significant effect on investment decision behavior (t = 4.821, p = .000) (Table 2, p. 50).
- Financial knowledge has a positive and significant effect on investment decision behavior (t = 3.540, p = .001) (Table 2, p. 50).
- Financial literacy has a positive and significant effect on investment decision behavior (t = 2.196, p = .030) (Table 2, p. 50).
- All four predictors simultaneously have a positive and significant effect on investment decision behavior (F = 998.78, p = .000) (Table 2, p. 50).
- The regression model reports R = 0.990 and R² = 0.980 (Table 2, p. 50).
- The regression equation is Investment Decision Behavior = 1.018 + 0.069X1 + 0.538X2 + 0.150X3 + 0.160X4 + e (Sect. 4.3, p. 50).
- Financial behavior has the largest unstandardized coefficient (B = .538), followed by financial literacy (B = .160), financial knowledge (B = .150) and financial attitude (B = .069) (Table 2, p. 50).

## Definitions

- **Financial attitude** — "how someone feels about the problems they face regarding personal finances, as measured by responses to a statement or opinion (Marsh, 2006)" (Sect. 2.2, p. 48).
- **Financial behavior** — "the ability of individuals to manage (planning, checking, budgeting, managing, controlling, storing and searching) financial funds on a daily basis" (Sect. 2.3, p. 48).
- **Financial knowledge** — "one's mastery over various matters related to finance and financial skills" (Sect. 2.4, p. 48).
- **Financial literacy** — "the ability to understand financial conditions and financial concepts and to transform that knowledge appropriately into behavior" (Sect. 2.5, p. 48).
- **Investment decision behavior** — "individual and depend entirely on independent individuals" (Sect. 2.6, p. 49).
- **Planned behavior theory** — individual behavior arises from the intention to behave, predicted by attitude toward the behavior, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control (Sect. 2.1, p. 48).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The authors acknowledge that the research is limited to financial attitude, financial behavior, financial knowledge and financial literacy, and that there are still many influencing factors including external factors of motivation, environment, and profit expectations that future researchers could use (Sect. 6, p. 52).
- The authors state that the research focuses on young investors, which is a differentiator from other studies that have not separated the level of investors based on demographics (Sect. 1, p. 48).
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not report confidence intervals for any regression coefficient, nor does it report standard errors for the F-statistic or R².
- [unacknowledged] The sample of 110 is derived from a minimum sample rule (5 × 22 indicators), but no margin of error, power analysis, or response rate is reported (Sect. 3, p. 49).
- [unacknowledged] The paper reports a Durbin-Watson value of 1.910 and states both "there is no autocorrelation" and "autocorrelation is negative" (Sect. 4.2, p. 50); these statements are contradictory.
- [unacknowledged] The paper reports the F-value as 998.789 in the text (Sect. 4.3, p. 50) but as 998.78 in Table 2 (p. 50), a discrepancy.
- [unacknowledged] The paper labels the simultaneous hypothesis as "hypothesis four (H5)" (Sect. 4.3, p. 50), a numbering error.
- [unacknowledged] The R² of 0.980 is implausibly high for cross-sectional survey data with 110 respondents and four predictors, suggesting possible overfitting or a reporting error.
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not report the questionnaire items themselves, the response scale, or descriptive statistics (means, standard deviations) for any variable.
- [unacknowledged] The paper does not report how the 110 respondents were recruited, whether sampling was random or convenience, or what the response rate was.
- [unacknowledged] The paper's population is 375 thousand young investors, but the sample is 110; no margin of error is reported to justify this sample size against the population.
- [unacknowledged] The paper cites a financial literacy figure of 38.03% (Sect. 1, p. 47) but also cites 21.84% for 2013 and 20% for 2014 (Sect. 1, p. 47); the year and source for the 38.03% figure are not given.
- [unacknowledged] The study is cross-sectional, so no causal direction can be established between the four predictors and investment decision behavior, despite the regression language of "effect."

## Remember This

- The paper finds all four financial attributes — attitude, behavior, knowledge, literacy — positively and significantly predict young investors' investment decision behavior in Indonesia.
- Financial attitude has the strongest t-value (7.795), financial behavior the largest B coefficient (.538), and financial literacy the weakest t-value (2.196).
- The model reports R = 0.990 and R² = 0.980, based on 110 respondents aged 18-25.
- The regression equation is: Investment Decision Behavior = 1.018 + 0.069X1 + 0.538X2 + 0.150X3 + 0.160X4 + e.
- The paper is a correlational survey study, not an experiment or an app evaluation; it reports no confidence intervals and no descriptive statistics.
