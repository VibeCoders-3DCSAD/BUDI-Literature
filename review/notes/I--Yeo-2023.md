# Financial Planning Behaviour: A Systematic Literature Review and New Theory Development

`I--Yeo-2023` — Yeo (2023), *Journal of Financial Services Marketing, 29, 979-1001* \[10.1057/s41264-023-00249-1]

## Summary

A SPAR-4-SLR systematic review of 30 Scopus- and Web of Science-indexed articles contextualises the theory of planned behaviour into a new theory of financial planning behaviour in which financial satisfaction, financial socialisation, and financial literacy with mental accounting and financial cognition drive intention and actual adoption.

## Problem and Motivation

Personal finance theories are borrowed rather than created, and existing frameworks remain piecemeal, not covering the whole spectrum of financial planning (p. 2). The authors argue the field has matured enough for theory development and that financial planning behaviour has had none of its own (p. 2).

## Method

**Design.** Systematic literature review (theory-based, for new theory development) following the SPAR-4-SLR protocol, followed by conceptual theory development; no other design label is stated (p. 7).
**Sample.** 30 articles included of 71 returned (unit: academic journal article), drawn from behavioural finance and personal finance studies published 2000-2020 and indexed in Scopus or Web of Science; the Appendix 1 header reports an aggregate sample of n = 43,361 across the 30 articles (pp. 8, 17).
**Context.** geography: multinational review; the reviewed studies are set in Australia, Cambodia, Canada, Germany, Italy, Malaysia, Oman, Pakistan, Qatar, the United States and unspecified others (Appendix 1, pp. 17-19); population: students, householders, investors, taxpayers, retirees, employees, business owners and debt-management clients; setting: secondary literature analysis, no new data collection.

- Appraise the theoretical traditions in financial planning — theory of reasoned action, TPB, Maslow's hierarchy of needs, life-cycle hypothesis and its behavioural enrichment, prospect theory, and the financial capability model (pp. 3-4).
- Select TPB as the most parsimonious lens on grounds of its organising capacity and its record of theory spin-offs such as the theory of behavioural control (p. 4).
- Set the review question as how the TPB can be contextualised to develop a theory of financial planning behaviour (p. 9).
- Fix the source quality as Scopus or Web of Science indexing and the search mechanism as Google Scholar, cross-checked manually against Scimago Journal Ranks and the Web of Science Master Journal List (p. 9).
- Run four keyword combinations across American and British spellings — 'financial planning' or 'personal financial planning' with 'theory of planned behavior' or 'theory of planned behaviour' — over 2000-2020 (p. 9), returning 71 articles (p. 8).
- Exclude 41 articles as duplicates, irrelevant, inaccessible or not journal articles, leaving 30 (p. 8).
- Organise the corpus with the ADO framework of Paul and Benito (2018) and Pansari and Kumar (2017) and the TCM framework of Paul et al. (2017) (p. 9).
- Content-analyse the 30 articles following Kraus et al. (2022), coded by one author and cross-validated by another with an intercoder reliability of ± 95% and differences clarified and resolved (p. 9).
- Develop the new theory by conceptual extrapolation and sensemaking (p. 9).
- Contextualise each TPB construct against the review findings in Table 2 and Fig. 3, substituting financial variables for the original components (p. 10).

Not reported.

## Key Findings

- num: The review covers 30 articles, of which the quantitative approach accounts for n = 24, the qualitative approach n = 3, the mixed method approach n = 1 and the conceptual approach n = 2 (p. 10).
- num: 71 articles were returned by the search and 41 were excluded, leaving 30 (Fig. 1, p. 8).
- num: The Appendix 1 header reports n = 2,898 citations across the 30 articles (p. 17), while Sect. Results states the combination of TPB and financial planning carries over 3000 citations among the same 30 articles (p. 10).
- num: The aggregate sample across the 30 articles is reported as n = 43,361 (Appendix 1 header, p. 17).
- num: Intercoder reliability for the content analysis was ± 95%, with differences clarified and resolved (p. 9).
- num: The search covered 20 years, from 2000 to 2020 (p. 9).
- num: The reviewed evidence is dominated by investment planning and cash flow planning components, with tax planning appearing in only two of the 30 studies (Appendix 1, pp. 17-19).
- num: Individual reviewed samples range from 118 participants among German family firms (Koropp et al., 2014) to 16,406 nonretired households (Magwegwe and Lim, 2020) (Appendix 1, pp. 17-18).
- num: Personal finance accounted for close to 250 publications in Scopus by the end of 2022 (p. 2, footnote 1).
- TPB is reported as a good predictor of financial planning behaviour, with attitude, subjective norms, perceived behavioural control and behavioural intention possessing positive relationships with each of the six planning components (p. 10).
- Behavioural intention does not always translate into behavioural performance, which is the reason the TPB adds perceived behavioural control (p. 6).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The new theory has not been empirically examined in its entirety, so its reliability, validity and generalisability are unestablished (Sect. Limitations and future research directions, p. 16).
- The review was limited to a single theoretical lens, TPB, while several other theories also explain financial planning behaviour (p. 16).
- The outcomes of financial planning have not been theorised; the assumption that good planning produces financial resilience still requires empirical verification (p. 16).
- The relationships in the new theory are inherently linear, and a cyclical reinforcing loop may exist that the theory cannot represent (p. 16).
- Financial planning behaviour as a research area remains in its infancy, so the theory was limited to the available evidence (p. 16).
- Financial counselling and financial therapy were outside the search terms and remain unexamined (p. 16).
- [unacknowledged] The corpus is restricted to 30 TPB-focused studies published 2000-2020, and the Appendix 1 sample column reads `NA` for four of them, so no pooled effect size can be derived.
- [unacknowledged] The review reports counts and narrative findings only: no effect sizes, confidence intervals, quality appraisal of the primary studies, or risk-of-bias assessment is reported for any of the 30 studies.
- [unacknowledged] No Philippine study appears in Appendix 1; the identifiable samples are from Australia, Canada, Cambodia, Germany, India, Italy, Malaysia, Oman, Pakistan, Qatar and the United States.

## Definitions

- **Theory of financial planning behaviour** — The paper's proposed theory, obtained by substituting financial variables for the original components of the theory of planned behaviour (Abstract, p. 1).
- **Financial satisfaction (attitude)** — The contextualised attitude construct; Table 2 defines it as the individual's disposition determined by whether they are free from financial worry and happy with life (p. 12).
- **Financial socialisation (subjective norms)** — The contextualised subjective-norm construct; Table 2 defines it as learning relevant financial information and acquiring societal expectations through socialising with family, friends and society (p. 12).
- **Financial literacy, mental accounting and financial cognition (perceived behavioural control)** — The three contextualised control constructs; Table 2 treats them as the capability and knowledge needed to make financial decisions (p. 12).
- **Mental accounting** — The cognitive process of dividing assets into current income, current assets and future income accounts, plus mental budgeting (p. 12).
- **Financial cognition** — The capacity to make financial decisions, comprising financial attitude, risk attitude and financial knowledge (p. 13).
- **Financial resilience** — The state that good financial planning is assumed to produce and that personal financial planning safeguards (pp. 15-16).
- **Financial capability model** — Atkinson et al.'s (2007) model with five components: making ends meet, keeping track, planning ahead, choosing products and staying informed (p. 4).
- **SPAR-4-SLR** — The Scientific Procedures and Rationales for Systematic Literature Reviews protocol of Paul et al. (2021), adopted in place of PRISMA (pp. 7-9).
- **Intercoder reliability** — The agreement between the coding author and the cross-validating author, reported here as ± 95% (p. 9).

## Remember This

- SPAR-4-SLR review of 30 TPB-focused articles, from 71 retrieved and 41 excluded, covering 2000-2020.
- The new theory substitutes financial satisfaction, financial socialisation, and financial literacy with mental accounting and financial cognition for TPB's three antecedents.
- Adoption of planning takes six forms: cash flow, tax, investment, risk, estate and retirement planning.
- The paper states outcomes of financial planning have not been theorised, so its theory explains intention and adoption, not financial results.
- Appendix 1 reports n = 2,898 citations while the results text says over 3000 for the same 30 articles.
