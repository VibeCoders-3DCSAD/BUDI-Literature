# Financial Inclusion in the Philippines: Dashboard, As of Fourth Quarter 2023

## Summary

This is a statistical dashboard compiled by the Financial Inclusion Office of the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), reporting the state of financial inclusion in the Philippines as of the fourth quarter of 2023, with comparison points drawn from 2022 Q4, 2021, 2022 and 2019 depending on the indicator. It is an indicator compilation, not an analytical study: the document presents numbers, growth rates, percentage shares and definitions, and prints no inferential statistics of any kind.

The dashboard is organised into four measurement domains — **Access**, **Usage**, **Inclusive Digital Finance**, and **Other Financial Inclusion Data** — followed by two priority-sector sections (**Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises** and the **Agricultural Sector**) and a closing block of **Other Relevant Data** on geography, population, the economy, income and poverty, overseas Filipinos, the mobile market, internet and social media, and e-commerce.

Access indicators for 2023 Q4 count 45 universal and commercial banks, 42 thrift banks, 389 rural and cooperative banks, 6 digital banks and 73 electronic money issuers (EMIs), plus 83 banks with e-banking. The physical and agent footprint comprises 13,307 banking offices, 23,349 ATMs, 95,053 cash and e-money agents, and 222,565 total financial service access points, or 27.91 access points per 10,000 adults. Of 1,634 cities and municipalities, 1,380 (84.5%) have banking presence and 1,572 (96.2%) have at least one access point; 62 (3.8%) have no access point at all.

Usage indicators report 121.6 million deposit accounts, 112.3 million depositors, 19.0 trillion pesos in deposits and 12.4 trillion pesos in loans in 2023 Q4. Microfinance lending through the banking system covered 1,995,276 microfinance borrowers and 1,723,340 microenterprise-loan borrowers. MSME loans totalled 503.0 billion pesos (6.3% of total business loans) and agriculture, forestry and fishing loans 283.0 billion pesos (2.13% of total loans outstanding).

Inclusive Digital Finance reports 24.2 million basic deposit accounts across 156 banks, 393.6 million registered and 70.1 million active e-money accounts, and digital payments reaching 53% of transaction volume and 55% of value in 2023. PESONet processed 91.1 million transactions worth 7,842.4 billion pesos and InstaPay 838.6 million transactions worth 5,021.5 billion pesos.

Demand-side survey data report 56% of adults with a formal account in 2021, 37% with savings, and 45% with outstanding loans. Household savings stood at 29.1% in 2023 Q4, of which 73.5% was held in a bank. The dashboard reports no confidence intervals, no p-values, no standard errors and no causal or predictive analysis; every cell is a count, an amount, a percentage or a growth rate.

## Method

**Design.** Descriptive indicator compilation; the paper states no formal design label. It is a quarterly monitoring dashboard that reports point-in-time counts and percentage shares with year-on-year or period-on-period growth, without a sampling frame of its own, without hypothesis testing, and without an analytical model.
**Sample.** Not reported as a single N; the unit of analysis is the reported financial-inclusion indicator, and each indicator carries its own population — individual adults aged 15 and over (BSP Financial Inclusion Survey, World Bank Findex), households (BSP Consumer Finance Survey, BSP Consumer Expectations Survey), or administrative counts of institutions, offices, accounts, agents, borrowers and transactions (BSP supervisory data, Insurance Commission, Cooperative Development Authority, Securities and Exchange Commission).
**Context.** geography: Philippines (national aggregates; no sub-national breakdown is printed). population: Filipino adults aged 15 and over, Filipino households, and BSP-supervised financial institutions with their offices, agents and accounts. setting: A national statistical dashboard compiled by the BSP Financial Inclusion Office, drawing on BSP survey and supervisory data plus World Bank Findex, PSA, IC, CDA, SEC, GSMA and We Are Social sources; the reporting period is the fourth quarter of 2023 with comparison points spanning 2019 to 2023.

### Structure and coverage

The Access block has four sub-parts: Banks and Electronic Money Issuers; Electronic Banking; Service Points of Banks and EMIs; and Other Financial Service Providers, closing with a Total Financial Service Access Points line and an Overall Access Situation table of cities and municipalities. The Usage block covers deposits and loans in the banking system, microfinance inside and outside the banking system, the credit information system, financing and lending companies, insurance and microinsurance, and investment (PERA and UITFs). The Inclusive Digital Finance block covers basic deposit accounts, e-money accounts, digital onboarding (e-KYC), digital access, digital payments, and alternative finance. The Other Financial Inclusion Data block is built entirely from demand-side surveys and is organised under Formal Account, Savings, Credit, Other Financial Services, Financial Resilience and Financial Literacy.

### Measurement notes the dashboard itself prints

- Access-point counts include banking offices, e-money and cash agents, NSSLAs, pawnshops, MSBs, other NBFIs, microfinance NGOs and cooperatives with credit operations [footnote 9, p. 9].
- Cash and e-money agents are the total number of active agents based on banks implementing or piloting the cash agent model under BSP Circular No. 940; the count excludes service points [footnote 3, p. 9].
- Following the AFI Core Set definition, "other access points" exclude ATMs (no data on ATMs that perform both cash-in and cash-out) and insurance providers (no data on location) [footnote 12, p. 9].
- The indicator "with banking presence" includes cash agents; on traditional metrics (banking offices only) the figures would be 1,153 (70.6%) for Q4 2022 and 1,162 (71.11%) for Q4 2023 [footnote 10, p. 9].
- Location data used to estimate the overall access situation are of different vintages: e-money and cash agents as of 2019; insurance companies as of 2020; lending and financing companies as of 2020 [footnote 11, p. 9].
- Microfinance NGO figures come only from a sample of NGOs that responded to the BSP data request; 21 accredited microfinance NGOs existed as of 25 October 2023 [footnote 8, p. 9].
- Of 20,752 operating cooperatives (head offices) as of December 2023, approximately 8,888 offer financial services [footnote 6, p. 9].
- MSME credit allocation reporting is for monitoring only, because the mandatory allocation lapsed in June 2018 [footnote 23, p. 9].
- Agri-Agra compliance reporting stopped with the period ending 30 September 2022 under Circular No. 1159 Series of 2022 [footnote 24, p. 9].

## Key Findings

- 1. Bank counts are contracting at the lower tiers while the EMI count grows: universal and commercial banks held at 45, thrift banks fell from 43 to 42, and rural and cooperative banks fell from 403 to 389, while EMIs rose from 69r/ to 73 (Access, p. 1).
- 2. The agent network expanded sharply, with cash and e-money agents rising from 62,699 in 2022 to 95,053 in 2023, a growth of 51.6%, even as ATMs declined slightly from 23,419 to 23,349 (Access, p. 1).
- 3. Access-point density fell even as the absolute count rose: 222,565 access points in 2023 Q4 against 222,301r/ in 2022 Q4 (+0.1%), but 27.91 per 10,000 adults against 28.33 (-1.5%) (Access, p. 1).
- 4. Almost the whole country is covered: 1,572 of 1,634 cities and municipalities (96.2%) have at least one access point, and only 62 (3.8%) have none (Access, p. 1).
- 5. Deposit mobilisation grew faster than depositor numbers, with accounts rising 19.7% to 121.6 million while depositors rose 9.8% to 112.3 million, and deposits rising 7.1% to 19.0 trillion pesos (Usage, p. 2).
- 6. Microfinance lending grew across every sub-product, with micro-agri loans up 45.9% in amount and micro-housing loan borrowers up 19.6% (Usage, p. 2).
- 7. Cooperatives outside the banking system grew strongly in 2022: deposit liabilities up 43.8% to 250.0 billion pesos and outstanding loans up 28.9% to 366.6 billion pesos (Usage, p. 2).
- 8. Insurance penetration fell from 1.73% to 1.60% of GDP between 2022 Q4 and 2023 Q4, a change of -0.13 (Usage, p. 2).
- 9. Basic deposit accounts grew fast from a small base: accounts up 57.6% to 24.2 million and the deposit amount up 206.6% to 36.7 billion pesos (Inclusive Digital Finance, p. 3).
- 10. Registered e-money accounts reached 393.6 million against 70.1 million active accounts, a 44.7% and 50.6% rise respectively (Inclusive Digital Finance, p. 3).
- 11. E-money transaction volumes exploded: inflows up 785.3% to 192.3 million and outflows up 859.7% to 440.2 million (Inclusive Digital Finance, p. 3).
- 12. Digital payments passed the halfway mark in 2023, at 53% of volume and 55% of value, up from 42% and 40% in 2022 (Inclusive Digital Finance, p. 3).
- 13. Demand-side account ownership rose from 29% in 2019 to 56% in 2021, but the growth came almost entirely from e-money accounts, which rose from 8% to 36%, while microfinance institution accounts fell from 12% to 9% (Demand-side Surveys, p. 4).
- 14. The share of adults with savings fell from 53% in 2019 to 37% in 2021, and 52% of those with savings kept them at home in 2021 (Demand-side Surveys, p. 4).
- 15. Household savings indicators weakened slightly in 2023 Q4: 29.1% of households had savings (from 30.5%) and 73.5% of those kept them in a bank (from 76.8%) (Demand-side Surveys, p. 4).
- 16. OFW households allocated more of their remittances to savings (35.2%, up from 29.3%), to debt payments (21.1%, up from 16.2%) and to investment (10.1%, up from 6.5%) (Demand-side Surveys, pp. 4-5).
- 17. Borrowing rose: 45% of adults had outstanding loans in 2021 against 33% in 2019, but only 4% borrowed from a bank and 57% borrowed from informal sources (Demand-side Surveys, p. 4).
- 18. MSME loans reached 503.0 billion pesos in 2023 Q4, 6.3% of total business loans and 4.1% of total banking-system loans, with 1,518,413 MSME borrowers (MSME Loans, p. 6).
- 19. MSME compliance with the lapsed mandatory allocation remained below the required levels: 1.9% for MSEs (required 8%) and 2.9% for MEs (required 2%) (MSME Loans, p. 6).
- 20. AFF loans reached 283.0 billion pesos, 2.13% of total loans outstanding, with AGRI compliance at 9.5% (required 15%) and AGRA compliance at 0.8% (required 10%) (AFF Loans, p. 6).
- 21. The dashboard prints no uncertainty measure for any indicator: no confidence interval, no standard error, no significance test and no variance appears anywhere in the document.

## Limitations and Gaps

- The dashboard is purely descriptive and prints no confidence intervals, standard errors, significance tests or variance for any of its indicators, so no reported difference can be assessed for statistical reliability from this document alone.
- Survey years are inconsistent across indicators. The BSP Financial Inclusion Survey figures are from 2019 and 2021; the World Bank Findex figures are from 2017 and 2021; the Consumer Finance Survey from 2014 and 2018; the Consumer Expectations Survey from 2022 Q4 and 2023 Q4; the cooperative data from 2021 and 2022. A reader cannot form a single-year picture of demand-side financial inclusion.
- The base populations differ between sources: BSP Financial Inclusion Survey and World Bank Findex report on adults aged 15 and over, while the BSP Consumer Finance Survey and Consumer Expectations Survey report on households. Percentages from these sources are not directly comparable.
- The access-point estimate mixes location vintages: e-money and cash agents as of 2019, insurance companies as of 2020, and lending and financing companies as of 2020 [footnote 11, p. 9]. The 2023 Q4 overall access situation therefore rests partly on location data up to four years old.
- Microfinance NGO figures are drawn from a sample of respondents to a BSP data request, not from the full population of 21 accredited microfinance NGOs [footnote 8, p. 9].
- ATMs and insurance providers are excluded from the "other access points" definition because the dashboard has no data on dual cash-in/cash-out ATMs and no location data for insurance providers [footnote 12, p. 9].
- MSME credit allocation has been reported for monitoring only since the mandatory allocation lapsed in June 2018 [footnote 23, p. 9], and Agri-Agra reporting stopped with the period ending 30 September 2022 [footnote 24, p. 9]; both series are therefore administratively discontinuous.
- The "with banking presence" indicator includes cash agents, which lifts coverage from 71.11% (banking offices only) to 84.5% for 2023 Q4 [footnote 10, p. 9]. The headline coverage figure is definition-dependent and the dashboard says so.
- [unacknowledged] Number of deposit accounts (121.6 million) exceeds number of depositors (112.3 million) for 2023 Q4 (Usage, p. 2). The dashboard prints both without noting that the gap implies multiple accounts per depositor or explaining the divergence.
- [unacknowledged] Registered e-money accounts (393.6 million) exceed the entire adult population of the Philippines (79.7 million, Other Relevant Data, p. 7) by roughly five times, and exceed total population (112.9 million) by more than three times. The dashboard reports registered and active accounts side by side but offers no account of dormancy, duplicate registration or the definitional basis for "registered".
- [unacknowledged] The 2023 Q4 access-points-per-10,000-adults figure of 27.91 does not exactly match the printed inputs (222,565 access points divided by 79.7 million adults gives approximately 27.92); the 2022 Q4 figure of 28.33 also sits slightly above the printed inputs (222,301r/ divided by 78.5 million gives approximately 28.32). The dashboard prints no denominator note.
- [unacknowledged] Insurance penetration is labelled "Change" rather than "Growth" and moves from 1.73% to 1.60% (Usage, p. 2), but the decline is not discussed or explained anywhere in the document.
- [unacknowledged] No indicator is broken down by region, province, income decile or urban/rural split except within the BSP Financial Inclusion Survey's account-ownership table (Demand-side Surveys, p. 4), so sub-national variation is invisible in every other block.
- [unacknowledged] The dashboard's "Financial Resilience" and "Financial Literacy" tables (p. 5) are drawn from the 2021 World Bank Findex and the 2021 BSP Financial Inclusion Survey; no 2023 measurement of either is printed, so the two most behaviourally relevant blocks are the oldest data in the document.
- [unacknowledged] The financial literacy block reports that only 2% of adults correctly answered all six financial literacy questions and that attendance at a financial literacy session was 7% (p. 5), but the dashboard draws no connection between these figures and any access, usage or savings indicator.

## Definitions

- **BDA (Basic Deposit Account)** — a low-cost deposit account; number of banks offering BDA, number of BDAs and amount of BDA deposits are reported for 2022 Q4 and 2023 Q4 (p. 3).
- **EMI (Electronic Money Issuer)** — counted among access providers, 73 in 2023 Q4 (p. 1).
- **BSFI (BSP-supervised financial institution)** — the reporting population for digital onboarding capability; 61 BSFIs had digital onboarding capability in 2023 Q4 (p. 3).
- **e-KYC / digital onboarding** — the capability of a BSFI to onboard customers digitally; reported as a count of BSFIs (p. 3).
- **NSSLA (Non-stock savings and loan association)** — counted among other financial service providers, 192 head offices and branches in 2023 Q4 (p. 1).
- **MSB (Money Service Business)** — counted among other financial service providers, 7,358 in 2023 Q4 (p. 1).
- **MSME** — micro, small and medium enterprises; MSME loans and MSME borrowers are reported under Priority Sectors (p. 6).
- **BMBE (Barangay Microbusiness Enterprise)** — a category of borrower; 3,402 BMBE borrowers in 2023 Q4 (p. 6).
- **AFF (Agriculture, Forestry and Fishing)** — the sector for AFF loan reporting (p. 6).
- **Agri-Agra** — the mandatory Agri-Agra credit allocation; AGRA and AGRI compliance are reported separately, with required levels of 10% and 15% respectively (p. 6).
- **PERA (Personal Equity & Retirement Account)** — a voluntary retirement savings vehicle; number of administrators, BSFIs offering PERA UITFs, approved products, contributors and total contributions are reported (p. 2).
- **UITF (Unit Investment Trust Fund)** — both PERA UITFs and existing non-PERA UITFs are counted, along with UITF participants (p. 2).
- **PESONet and InstaPay** — the two digital payment streams whose volume and value are reported (p. 3).
- **BSP Financial Inclusion Survey (FIS)** — the demand-side survey of adults aged 15 and over (p. 4).
- **World Bank Findex** — the international demand-side survey of adults aged 15 and over used for the formal-account, savings, credit, other-financial-services and financial-resilience blocks (pp. 4-5).

## Key Figures and Tables

The dashboard contains no numbered figures or tables. Its reporting blocks, with locators, are:

- **Access** (p. 1): bank and EMI counts; banks with e-banking; banking offices; ATMs (on-site and off-site); cash and e-money agents; insurance providers (insurers and microinsurance providers); other financial service providers; total financial service access points and access points per 10,000 adults; overall access situation by city and municipality.
- **Usage** (p. 2): deposits and loans in the banking system; microfinance in the banking system by product (microfinance, microenterprise loans, microfinance plus, micro-agri loans, micro-housing loans); microfinance outside the banking system (cooperatives, microfinance NGOs); credit information system; financing and lending companies; insurance and microinsurance coverage and premiums; investment (PERA and UITFs).
- **Inclusive Digital Finance** (p. 3): basic deposit accounts; e-money accounts (registered, active) and transaction volumes and amounts (inflow, outflow); digital onboarding (e-KYC); digital access; digital payments (PESONet, InstaPay, and the digital share of total payment transactions); alternative finance (crowdfunding, crowdinvesting, crowdlending).
- **Other Financial Inclusion Data** (pp. 4-5): Formal Account; Savings; Credit; Other Financial Services; Financial Resilience; Financial Literacy.
- **Priority Sectors** (p. 6): MSMEs — business establishments, jobs employed, contribution to gross value added, men versus women-owned/led MSMEs, MSME loans, MSME loans under RA 6977; Agricultural Sector — share to total agricultural production, year-on-year growth by subsector, AFF loans, Agri-Agra compliance.
- **Other Relevant Data** (pp. 7-8): Geography; Population; Economy; Income and Poverty; Overseas Filipinos; Mobile Market; Internet and Social Media; E-Commerce.

## Remember This

- This is a descriptive national dashboard, not an analytical study: it prints no confidence intervals, no p-values, no standard errors and no causal claims anywhere.
- Access in 2023 Q4: 222,565 total access points, 27.91 per 10,000 adults, 95,053 cash and e-money agents, 23,349 ATMs, and 96.2% of cities and municipalities with at least one access point.
- Usage in 2023 Q4: 121.6 million deposit accounts, 112.3 million depositors, 19.0 trillion pesos in deposits and 12.4 trillion pesos in loans.
- Digital finance in 2023: 393.6 million registered but only 70.1 million active e-money accounts, and digital payments at 53% of volume and 55% of value.
- Demand-side savings are thin and mostly outside banks: 37% of adults had savings in 2021, and 52% of savers kept them at home.
- Borrowing is common but bank borrowing is rare: 45% of adults had outstanding loans in 2021, only 4% borrowed from a bank, and 57% borrowed from informal sources.
- The access-point estimate blends location data of different vintages (2019 for e-money and cash agents, 2020 for insurance and for lending and financing companies).
