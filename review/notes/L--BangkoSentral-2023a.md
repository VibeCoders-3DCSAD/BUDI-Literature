# L--BangkoSentral-2023b — Extraction Note

## Summary

This is the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas's annual *Report on Regional Economic Developments in the Philippines* for 2023. It compiles national and sub-national statistics on real-sector output, agriculture, construction, local government receipts and expenditures, inflation, approved investments, banking, microfinance, MSME and agri-agra lending, and business and household expectations. The Philippine economy grew 5.5 percent in 2023, down from 7.6 percent in 2022, with all seventeen regions posting positive growth. Headline inflation rose to 6.0 percent, breaching the 2.0–4.0 percent target in every region, driven mainly by rice prices. The banking sector remained sound, with total loans up 7.9 percent, deposit liabilities up 7.1 percent, and the loans-to-deposits ratio at 64.8 percent. Digital savings accounts more than doubled to 8.5 million by December 2023. Microfinance loans grew 13.4 percent to P32.0 billion, MSME loans grew 1.8 percent to P502.2 billion, and agri-agra loans grew 1.5 percent to P944.4 billion. The Consumer Expectations Survey shows a more pessimistic consumer outlook in Q4 2023, with the overall confidence index at -19.0 percent, and reports household savings, debt, and OFW remittance-allocation behaviour. The report is descriptive and compilative: it contains no causal estimates, no confidence intervals and no significance tests.

## Method

**Design.** The paper states no formal design label; it is an annual institutional economic report that compiles sub-national administrative statistics and quarterly BSP survey results for 2021–2023.
**Sample.** No single study sample: the report aggregates annual data for all 17 Philippine regions, supported by the quarterly Business Expectations Survey (firms drawn from the Top 7,000 Corporations list) and the quarterly Consumer Expectations Survey (random sample of about 5,000 households).
**Context.** geography: Philippines, all 17 regions; population: Philippine households, firms, local government units, banks, and microfinance/MSME/agri-agra borrowers; setting: national and regional economic surveillance using PSA, BSP-DES, BSP-DSA and DBM administrative data, with no fieldwork or experiment conducted by the authors.

## Key Findings

- num: The Philippine economy grew 5.5 percent in 2023, lower than the 7.6 percent growth in 2022 (Introduction, p. 1).
- num: All 17 regions posted positive economic performance in 2023, led by Central Visayas (7.3 percent), Western Visayas (7.2 percent) and Ilocos Region (7.1 percent) (Key Regional Developments, p. 4; Table 1, Gross Regional Domestic Product).
- num: Headline inflation rose to 6.0 percent in 2023 from 5.8 percent in 2022, with all regions breaching the upper bound of the 2.0–4.0 percent target (Introduction, p. 1).
- num: Total loans increased by 7.9 percent in 2023, and the nationwide loans-to-deposits ratio rose to 64.8 percent from 64.3 percent (Introduction, p. 1).
- num: Digital savings accounts at the six digital banks reached 8.5 million by December 2023, a 103.8 percent increase from 4.2 million in June 2023 (Introduction, p. 1; Table 2, Digital Account Ownership, p. 17).
- num: Total microfinance loans grew 13.4 percent to P32.0 billion; total MSME loans grew 1.8 percent to P502.2 billion; total agri-agra loans grew 1.5 percent to P944.4 billion (Key Regional Developments, pp. 17-19).
- num: The overall consumer outlook index turned more negative at -19.0 percent in Q4 2023 from -9.6 percent in Q3 2023 (Key Regional Developments, p. 23).
- num: The percentage of households that availed a loan in the last 12 months was higher in AONCR (24.0 percent) than in NCR (15.8 percent) (Key Regional Developments, p. 24).
- The report is descriptive and compilative; it reports no causal estimates, confidence intervals or significance tests.

## Limitations and Gaps

- The authors caution that, given the number of survey responses received, BES sectoral and regional estimates should be interpreted/used with caution (footnote 1, Table 24, Business Expectations Survey, Statistical Annexes).
- The authors note that regional aggregation of MSME loans and agri-agra loans is based on banks' Head Office locations and does not reflect actual transactions made in a specific region (notes to Tables 20–23, Statistical Annexes).
- The authors note that details in the Consumer Expectations Survey may not add up to totals due to rounding (Table 25, Consumer Expectations Survey, Statistical Annexes).
- The authors note that respondent firms reporting 0 percent capacity utilization were included in the ACU computation starting with the Q2 2023 BES report, and that Q1 2023 BES ACU figures were revised to reflect this update (footnote 3, Table 24, Statistical Annexes).
- [unacknowledged] The report is descriptive and compilative; it reports no confidence intervals, standard errors, p-values or causal estimates for any of its quantities.
- [unacknowledged] No response rates are reported for the BES or CES, and the CES is described only by its national target of about 5,000 households, with no per-region respondent counts.
- [unacknowledged] Coverage is annual 2021–2023 only; no monthly or seasonal series is reported, so seasonal expense patterns cannot be estimated from this source.
- [unacknowledged] The report does not discuss personal financial management applications, budgeting tools, or software-supported planning.
- [unacknowledged] All quantities are aggregates; no individual- or household-level microdata are reported, so income–saving–resilience relationships cannot be estimated.
- [unacknowledged] Several narrative figures contradict the annex tables by small margins (e.g., the text describes microfinance-loan growth of 13.4 percent while Table 19 prints 13.4; the text says MSME loans were "P502.2 billion" while Table 21 prints P502,208.75 million); the report does not reconcile them.

## Remember This

- This is the BSP's 2023 Report on Regional Economic Developments, an institutional compilation of national and sub-national statistics for 2021–2023.
- The Philippine economy grew 5.5 percent in 2023, down from 7.6 percent in 2022; headline inflation was 6.0 percent and all regions breached the 2.0–4.0 percent target.
- The report documents household savings and debt behaviour, and OFW remittance-allocation behaviour, from the Consumer Expectations Survey.
- The report's data sources are PSA, BSP-DES, BSP-DSA and DBM administrative data, plus the BES and CES.
- The report is descriptive only: no confidence intervals, no p-values, no causal estimates, no seasonal or monthly series.
