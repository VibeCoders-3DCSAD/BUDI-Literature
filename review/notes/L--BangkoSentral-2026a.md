# Consumer Expectations Survey Report: 2nd Quarter 2026

`L--BangkoSentral-2026` — Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (2026), *Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas* \[Not reported]

## Summary

The BSP Consumer Expectations Survey for Q2 2026 reports that households turned more pessimistic overall due to higher fuel and food inflation from the Middle East conflict, graft and corruption concerns, and insufficient government support to cushion rising prices.

## Problem and Motivation

The survey tracks consumer confidence to gauge near-term household financial conditions and economic outlook. Rising prices and economic uncertainty shape household decisions about spending, saving and borrowing.

## Method

**Design.** Quarterly stratified multi-stage probability sample survey of households (consumer expectations survey).
**Sample.** Not applicable (institutional statistical report; no participant sample size defined in the text as an analytic sample in the academic sense).
**Context.** geography: Philippines (national), with NCR and AONCR breakdown; population: Households nationwide; setting: Field survey conducted by the BSP Department of Economic Statistics.

- Conducted stratified multi-stage probability sampling scheme with samples drawn from the Philippine Statistics Authority's 2023 Geo-Enabled Master Sample for household-based surveys (Survey Details, p. 2).
- Fieldwork conducted 6-18 April 2026 nationwide.
- Reports response rate 98.4 percent and margin of error ±1.3% (Philippines).
- Includes breakdowns by income group (low-, middle-, high-income) and by region (NCR, AONCR).
- Tracks diffusion indices for inflation rate, peso borrowing rate, exchange rate, and unemployment rate for current quarter, next quarter, and next 12 months.

## Key Findings

- num: Survey period 6-18 April 2026; sample size 5,503 households nationwide (NCR 2,476/45.0%, AONCR 3,027/55.0%); response rate 98.4%; margin of error ±1.3% (Survey Details, p. 2).
- num: In Q2 2026, overall consumer confidence was more pessimistic quarter-on-quarter; next quarter (Q3 2026) turned pessimistic; next 12 months less optimistic (Fig. 1, p. 1).
- num: Consumers less likely to save in Q2 2026, possibly because rising prices prompt allocating larger share of income to expenditures (p. 1).
- num: Households more cautious about borrowing in the next 12 months while expecting higher spending on basic needs; with expected rising unemployment, managing additional debt more difficult (p. 1).
- num: Households prioritize essential goods while cutting back on discretionary purchases as they became less optimistic about their financial situation (p. 1).
- num: More households intended not to purchase big-ticket items (consumer durables, motor vehicles, house and lot) in the next 12 months (p. 1).
- num: Percentage of households who would set aside savings in the current quarter: Q2 2026 was 49.6% (Philippines), down from Q1 2026 56.2%; by income group Q2 2026: Low-income 42.1%, Middle-income 47.3%, High-income 57.1% (Table 10, p. 9).
- num: Percentage of income allocated to savings (Philippines, Q2 2026): Less than 5% 47.4%; 5% to 9% 29.9%; 10% and over 22.8%; breakdown within 10%+ includes 10-14% 14.9%, 15-19% 3.4%, 20-24% 2.6%, 25%+ 1.9% (Table 10, p. 9).
- num: Low-income group allocation <5% was 58.1% in Q2 2026 (Table 10, p. 9).
- num: Consumers who expect higher inflation cited rising food and utility prices (1), insufficient government policies/programs to address higher inflation (2), and tighter supply conditions (3) (p. 2).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The report is an institutional consumer expectations survey focused on near-term sentiment and economic indicators; it does not present an academic study design, hypotheses, or inferential statistical tests beyond reported percentages/diffusion indices.
- The Chapter 2 claim that this paper reports "savings buffers are thin and expenditures track income closely (Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas [BSP], 2026b; Financial Sector Forum [FSCC], 2024)" is not supported by this Q2 2026 report (L--BangkoSentral-2026). The report does not contain explicit text stating households have "thin savings buffers" as a measured construct in that phrasing, and while it notes "expenditures track income closely" is cited via BSP 2026b in the thesis text, this specific document (2026) focuses on consumer expectations/sentiment rather than reporting household savings buffer measures in that form.
- [unacknowledged] The report notes methodological change starting Q1 2025: only forward-looking questions on household savings retained and the savings outlook question refined to better capture intention to save (Table 10 footnote 2). Comparisons across periods are affected by this change.

## Definitions

- **Consumer Expectations Survey (CES)** — BSP's quarterly household survey tracking consumer confidence and expectations about economic conditions, family financial situation, income, spending, saving, and borrowing.
- **Diffusion Index (DI)** — Index indicating direction of change (increase/decrease) in selected economic indicators based on respondent shares; +/- denotes more respondents expect increase/decrease.
- **Savings sentiment/savings intention** — Forward-looking indication of whether households would set aside savings in the current quarter (reported from Q1 2025 onward due to survey streamlining).
