# L--BangkoSentral-2026b — Q1 2026 Report on Economic and Financial Developments

## Summary

This is the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas' quarterly review of Philippine economic and financial developments for the first quarter of 2026, covering global conditions, the real sector, the monetary sector, the financial sector, and the external sector, plus a conclusion and two policy annexes. The report's headline narrative is a slowdown: real GDP grew 2.8 percent y-o-y in Q1 2026, down from 5.4 percent a year earlier and 3.0 percent in Q4 2025, on weaker domestic demand, subdued investment (notably public construction), and softer household spending, with the escalation of the Middle East conflict in late February 2026 adding a supply-side oil shock.

Inflation re-emerged but stayed inside the target band: headline inflation averaged 2.8 percent in Q1 2026, up from 1.7 percent in Q4 2025, driven by food (especially rice) and fuel prices, while official core inflation rose to 3.0 percent. The BSP cut the Target RRP rate by 25 bps to 4.25 percent on 19 February 2026, then held it unchanged at an off-cycle meeting on 26 March 2026 as inflation risks increased.

Fiscal consolidation continued: the NG deficit narrowed to 5.1 percent of GDP from 6.8 percent a year earlier on higher non-tax revenues (early GOCC dividend remittances) and slower disbursements, while the debt-to-GDP ratio reached 65.2 percent, its highest since 2006. The financial system remained resilient — banking resources reached ₱31.1 trillion (up 9.3 percent y-o-y, 109.8 percent of GDP), lending grew 10.7 percent, and CARs stayed above regulatory minimums — though GNPL ratios edged up and financial markets weakened (PSEi down 1.7 percent, CDS spreads wider, peso weaker). The external position deteriorated, with a BOP deficit of 4.5 percent of GDP, a narrower services surplus, and reserves falling to US$106.6 billion, partly buffered by strong exports, remittances, BPO receipts, and travel earnings.

## Problem and Motivation

The report is a monitoring document rather than a hypothesis-testing study: it exists to give the Monetary Board, policymakers, and the public a consolidated read on whether the Philippine economy and financial system are absorbing shocks. The motivating problem in Q1 2026 is a specific one — a geopolitical oil-price shock landing on an economy already slowing because of governance-related delays in public construction, weaker household demand, and a delayed 2026 budget. The report therefore tracks whether price stability, financial-sector soundness, and external liquidity are holding up under that combination, and it frames the policy question as balancing support for a slowing economy against the risk of second-round inflation effects.

## Method

**Design.** The report states no formal design label; it is a quarterly descriptive and analytical macro-financial review that compiles and interprets official statistics produced by the BSP, the Philippine Statistics Authority, the Bureau of the Treasury, the Philippine Stock Exchange, PDEx, and other agencies.
**Sample.** Not applicable in the sampling sense — the report uses census-style national aggregates rather than a sample: the full Philippine banking system (13,633 banking offices as of Q1 2026, Table 7), the National Government's cash operations (Table 3), and national balance-of-payments, reserves and external-debt accounts (Tables 11–15), with a unit of analysis of the Philippine economy and financial system for Q1 2026.
**Context.** geography: Philippines, with regional comparisons against Vietnam, Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, China, Thailand, and (for CDS, EMBIG and banking ratios) South Korea and ASEAN peers; population: the Philippine macroeconomy — households, firms, the National Government, the BSP-supervised financial system, and non-resident creditors and investors; setting: national macroeconomic and financial-system surveillance conducted from Manila on published administrative and survey data, with no fieldwork, no human participants, and no intervention by the authors.

## Key Findings

- Real GDP growth slowed to 2.8 percent y-o-y in Q1 2026 (5.4 percent in Q1 2025; 3.0 percent in Q4 2025), or 0.9 percent q-o-q seasonally adjusted (Executive Summary, p. 2; Sect. II.A.1, p. 6).
- The slowdown was demand-side and supply-side at once: gross capital formation contracted 3.3 percent, household final consumption grew only 3.0 percent — its weakest outside the pandemic in about 15 years — while agriculture (-0.2 percent) and industry (-0.1 percent) contracted and services grew 4.5 percent (Table 1 cont'd; Table 1a cont'd; Sect. II.A.1, p. 7).
- Headline inflation averaged 2.8 percent in Q1 2026, up from 1.7 percent in Q4 2025, with official core inflation at 3.0 percent; the BSP cut the Target RRP rate by 25 bps to 4.25 percent on 19 February 2026 and held it at the 26 March 2026 off-cycle meeting (Sect. II.B.1, p. 11; Sect. II.B.2, p. 12).
- The NG deficit narrowed to 5.1 percent of GDP from 6.8 percent, but the debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2 percent, its highest since 2006, driven by frontloaded issuances, peso-depreciation valuation effects, and ODA net availments (Sect. II.A.3, p. 10).
- Banking resources reached ₱31.1 trillion, up 9.3 percent y-o-y and equal to 109.8 percent of GDP, while U/KB lending grew 10.7 percent and M3 grew 12.1 percent to ₱20.4 trillion; GNPLs rose to 3.3 percent and NPL coverage fell to 91.4 percent (Sect. II.C.1, pp. 16–18; Sect. II.C.2, p. 19; Table A).
- Household balance sheets were a weak spot: consumption growth slowed, precautionary saving rose to a record saving intention index of 12.4, and household consumption loans rose to 15.6 percent of U/KB total loans, led by credit cards (27.9 percent) and motor vehicle loans (12.5 percent) (Sect. II.A.1, fn. 3, p. 7; Sect. II.C.1, p. 17; Sect. II.C.2, p. 19).
- Financial markets turned risk-averse: the PSEi fell 1.7 percent to 5,948.9, the P/E eased to 9.1 times, 5-year sovereign CDS widened to 91 bps from 57 bps, EMBIG Philippines widened to 86 bps from 59 bps, and the peso averaged ₱58.95/US$1 (Sect. II.C.4, pp. 20–23).
- The external position weakened: the BOP deficit widened to 4.5 percent of GDP from 2.6 percent, the goods trade deficit widened 4.7 percent to US$17.1 billion, financial account net inflows fell to US$2.4 billion from US$7.4 billion, and GIR declined to US$106.6 billion from US$110.8 billion (Sect. II.D.1–II.D.2, pp. 26–27; Table 11; Table 12).
- Offsetting buffers held: goods exports rose 13.6 percent to US$17.6 billion, net remittances rose 2.3 percent to US$7.9 billion, BPO revenues rose 1.3 percent to US$7.3 billion, the debt service ratio was 9.5 percent, and external debt fell slightly to US$147.4 billion (Sect. II.D.1, p. 26; Sect. II.D.3, p. 28; Table 15).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The report is a descriptive aggregate review with no causal identification and no inference statistics. No confidence interval, standard error, or significance test is printed for any headline change it describes — for example, the 2.8 percent versus 3.0 percent GDP growth comparison, the 5.1 percent versus 6.8 percent deficit ratio, or the 3.3 percent versus 3.1 percent GNPL ratio. `[unacknowledged]`
- The saving intention index (12.4 in Q1 2026) is an intentions measure drawn from the BSP Consumer Expectations Survey, not a measure of realised saving or of the adequacy of household savings buffers; the report never reports a savings-buffer or liquid-asset measure for Filipino households. `[unacknowledged]`
- The report gives no household-level microdata and no income-versus-expenditure tracking. Household consumption is reported only as a national-accounts aggregate (3.0 percent growth in Q1 2026), so nothing in the report tests whether expenditures track income closely at the household level. `[unacknowledged]`
- The authors acknowledge that the outlook is clouded: "the outlook remains clouded by uncertainty over the duration and intensity of the ME conflict, the risk of further oil-related inflation pressures, and the need to improve business sentiment" (Executive Summary, p. 3).
- The authors acknowledge fiscal fragility: "The margin remains fragile, as the ME conflict continues to exert supply-side pressures, weighing on growth while raising public financing costs" (Sect. II.A.3, p. 10).
- Several indicators carry preliminary (`p`) or revised (`r`) status in the statistical tables, so the Q1 2026 values reported here are subject to later revision; the report itself flags this in the table legends (Tables 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 9a, 11, 15). `[unacknowledged as a limitation on the narrative]`
- The report names Figures 1 through 32 in the text, but the extracted document reproduces only the figure captions and axis labels, not the underlying plotted series, so most chart-based values cannot be independently checked from this file. `[unacknowledged]`
- The report contains no analysis of personal financial management software, expense forecasting, anomaly detection, or budgeting tools of any kind; it contains no algorithm comparison, no usability evaluation, and no model performance metric. `[unacknowledged]`
- The statistical tables in the annex carry no printed page numbers in this conversion, so locators to Tables 1–15 are given by table number rather than page.

## Definitions

- **BSP** — Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas, the Philippine central bank and the report's publisher.
- **Target RRP rate** — the BSP's policy interest rate, set at 4.25 percent after the 19 February 2026 25-bp cut and held at the 26 March 2026 off-cycle meeting.
- **GNPL / NNPL ratio** — gross and net non-performing loans as a share of total loans; 3.3 percent and 1.5 percent respectively at end-March 2026.
- **M3** — broad money liabilities, comprising M2 plus deposit substitutes; ₱20.4 trillion at end-March 2026, up 12.1 percent y-o-y.
- **GIR** — gross international reserves; US$106.6 billion at end-March 2026, covering 7.0 months of imports.
- **STRM** — short-term external debt based on remaining maturity; 17.3 percent of total external debt at end-March 2026.
- **DSR** — debt service ratio, principal and interest payments relative to exports of goods and receipts from services and primary income; 9.5 percent for January–March 2026.
- **REER** — real effective exchange rate, the NEER adjusted for inflation differentials; 95.32 in Q1 2026, 0.6 percent below its HP-filtered trend.
- **PhilPaSSplus** — the BSP's payments and settlements system, which processed 436,086 transactions worth ₱165.1 trillion in Q1 2026.
- **Saving intention index** — a BSP Consumer Expectations Survey measure of households' intent to save; reached a record-high 12.4 in Q1 2026.
- **U/KBs** — universal and commercial banks, which held 92.8 percent of banking sector resources.
- **AONCR** — Areas Outside the National Capital Region.

## Remember This

- The BSP's Q1 2026 report is a macro-financial surveillance document, not an empirical study: no hypotheses, no sampling, no inference statistics, no treatment or intervention.
- Real GDP growth of 2.8 percent y-o-y in Q1 2026 was the weakest in the series shown, with investment contracting 3.3 percent and household consumption growing only 3.0 percent.
- Headline inflation averaged 2.8 percent (up from 1.7 percent) and core inflation 3.0 percent; the BSP cut the policy rate 25 bps to 4.25 percent in February, then held it in March.
- The NG deficit narrowed to 5.1 percent of GDP while the debt-to-GDP ratio rose to 65.2 percent, its highest since 2006.
- Banking stayed sound — ₱31.1 trillion in resources, 10.7 percent loan growth, 15.6/16.0 percent CARs — but GNPLs rose to 3.3 percent and NPL coverage fell to 91.4 percent.
- Household-level evidence in the report is limited to aggregate consumption growth, a record-high saving intention index of 12.4, and rising household consumption loans (15.6 percent of U/KB loans).
- The paper does not measure savings-buffer adequacy or income-expenditure tracking, and cites no personal financial management software or forecasting algorithm.
