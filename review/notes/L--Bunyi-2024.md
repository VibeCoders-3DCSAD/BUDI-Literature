# Unpacking the Determinants of Financial Resilience in the Philippines

`L--Bunyi-2024` — Bunyi (2024), *Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas Discussion Paper Series No. 03*

## Summary

Across the 2017 and 2021 World Bank Global Findex rounds, income quintile, saving behavior, and gender were the top predictors of Filipino financial resilience in every model, and demographics carried more predictive value than financial access; no model exceeded 70 percent test accuracy.

## Problem and Motivation

Financial resilience is not guaranteed by financial inclusion: South Africa reported 69.2 percent account ownership but only 28.7 percent resilience, while Vietnam showed the reverse (Sec. I, p. 3). The COVID-19 pandemic exposed household vulnerabilities, and Philippine policymakers need to know which characteristics identify the financially vulnerable in order to target interventions (Sec. I, pp. 3-4). To the author's knowledge, research on the determinants of financial resilience had yet to be conducted in the Philippine context (Sec. II, p. 6).

## Method

**Design.** Predictive modelling and inference study on secondary survey data; the paper labels its exercise "predictive models" and generates inferences on determinants, and states no separate formal design label (Secs. V-VI).
**Sample.** n = 980 respondents (2017) and 992 respondents (2021) after dropping missing-data cases from 1,000 interviewed each round; unit = one adult respondent aged 15 and above in the Philippine Global Findex survey (Sec. III, p. 7).
**Context.** geography: Philippines; population: adults aged 15 and above in nationally representative Global Findex samples; setting: secondary analysis of a household survey, no field site.

- Select 23 predictive attributes patterned after Salignac et al. (2019)'s Multidimensional Financial Resilience Framework — economic resources, financial resources, financial knowledge and behavior, social capital, and demographics — all present in both survey rounds (Sec. III, p. 7; Table 1).
- Drop observations where respondents refused to answer or did not know the answer to any selected attribute, trimming 1,000 to 980 (2017) and 1,000 to 992 (2021) (Sec. III, p. 7).
- Recode the resilience target for comparability: 2017 "possible"/"not possible", 2021 "not difficult at all"/"somewhat difficult"/"very difficult", treating little-to-no difficulty as resilient (Sec. III, pp. 7-8).
- Characterize the data by demographic profile before modelling (Sec. IV, pp. 8-14).
- Select Logistic/Logistic LASSO Regression and Decision Tree as the models suited to the classification task, robustness, redundancy and interaction concerns (Sec. V, p. 15).
- Run the models across hyper-parameters, choose those maximizing test-set accuracy, and use a 75/25 train-test split (Sec. VI, p. 16).
- Benchmark with Random Forest and Linear Discriminant Analysis, and also consider Naive Bayes, K-Nearest Neighbors, Neural Networks and Support Vector Machines (Sec. VI, pp. 16-17).
- Evaluate the models on accuracy, ROC curves, and confusion matrices that trade off sensitivity and specificity (Sec. VI, pp. 17-19).
- Select the models for inference on the highest true negatives and fewest false positives, prioritizing coverage of the vulnerable (Sec. VI, p. 19).
- Generate inferences from Logistic LASSO coefficients, decision-tree splits, and feature importance (Sec. VI, pp. 19-23).

## Key Findings

- num: Income quintile, saving behavior, and gender were the top predictors in both the 2017 and 2021 survey rounds (Abstract, p. 2).
- num: For 2017, the Decision Tree with minimum samples per split = 151 scored 64.1 percent test accuracy, while the Random Forest with maximum tree depth 3 and 1,500 estimators scored 65.3 percent (Table 5a, p. 17).
- num: For 2021, the Decision Tree with minimum samples per split = 99 topped accuracy at 68.1 percent (Sec. VI, p. 19; Table 5b, p. 17).
- num: Accuracy scores hovered in the 59-65 percent range for 2017 data and 62-68 percent for 2021 data, against base rates of 49.4 percent (2017) and 59.0 percent (2021) (Sec. VI, p. 16).
- num: The Logistic LASSO regressions found a strong positive relationship with income quintile, a moderate positive relationship with saving for retirement, and a moderate negative relationship with being female (Sec. VI, p. 19; Fig. 10).
- num: In 2017, age was strongly negatively associated with resilience but turned uninformative in 2021, while borrowing for medical purposes was uninformative in 2017 and strongly negative in 2021 (Sec. VI, pp. 19-20).
- num: Financial account ownership rose from 34.3 percent to 56.8 percent, mobile money account ownership from 4.4 percent to 30.2 percent, online purchases from 8.8 percent to 51.1 percent, and saving for retirement from 29.0 percent to 41.7 percent between 2017 and 2021 (Sec. IV, p. 13).
- num: The Findex found saving behavior rising from 59.6 percent to 64.8 percent, whereas the BSP Financial Inclusion Survey found adults with savings falling from 48 percent to 37 percent over the same period (Sec. IV, pp. 13-14).
- num: Resilience by sex was 46.1 percent (female) and 53.6 percent (male) in 2017, widening to 51.8 percent and 68.6 percent in 2021 (Tables 2a-2b, p. 11).
- num: z-score and interquartile range methods flagged one 2017 respondent aged 95 and four 2021 respondents aged 71 and above as outliers, which were kept (Sec. IV, p. 11).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The author acknowledges that "modest model accuracy coupled with other limitations of the dataset, such as the narrow proxy measure for financial resilience used in the Findex survey, may weigh on the validity of the findings," and every model stays below 70 percent accuracy (Sec. VII, p. 25).
- [unacknowledged] The paper calls the 2017 Decision Tree with at least 151 samples per split the model that "achieved the highest accuracy score", but Table 5a gives it 64.1 percent against the Random Forest's 65.3 percent, and the same page calls the Random Forest the best predictive score (Sec. VI, p. 19; Table 5a, p. 17).
- [unacknowledged] No inferential statistics are printed for any model: no coefficient values, standard errors, p-values or confidence intervals, so the strength and significance of the reported associations cannot be checked (Sec. VI, pp. 19-20; Figs. 10-12).
- [unacknowledged] The 2021 target variable was recoded from the 2017 "possible"/"not possible" question to a difficulty scale collapsed into resilient/vulnerable, so the two rounds do not measure resilience identically and cross-year comparisons rest on that recoding (Sec. III, p. 8).
- [unacknowledged] The 2021 round moved from face-to-face to phone-based enumeration, so respondents without phone access were excluded, which the author notes may limit generalizability and probably understates income and education gaps (Sec. IV, p. 14).
- [unacknowledged] Financial resilience is a binary self-report and the paper notes possible recall and withholding error, with no adjustment or robustness check reported (Sec. III, p. 7).
- [unacknowledged] The Findex increase in saving (59.6 to 64.8 percent) contradicts the BSP Financial Inclusion Survey decline in savings (48 to 37 percent), and the paper attributes the gap to question wording without resolving which measure is right (Sec. IV, pp. 13-14).
- [unacknowledged] The samples are small (980 and 992 observations) and the 23 predictors are predominantly binary, which the paper ties to the sub-70 percent accuracy but does not remedy (Sec. III, p. 7; Sec. VII, p. 25).

## Definitions

- **Financial resilience** — the ability to withstand unexpected income loss or financial shocks; operationalized in the Findex as the ability to gather emergency funds within the next month amounting to 5 percent of the country's GNI per capita.
- **Financial vulnerability** — over-indebtedness, falling behind on utility payments, insufficient discretionary income, and susceptibility to financial shocks.
- **Financial inclusion** — the accessibility, usage, and quality of financial products and services.
- **Global Findex** — the World Bank Global Financial Inclusion survey, taken from nationally representative samples of adults aged 15 and above from more than 140 economies.
- **Logistic LASSO Regression** — logistic regression with a LASSO (L1) shrinkage penalty that zeroes out irrelevant coefficient estimates.
- **Decision Tree** — a non-parametric technique that splits known cases into homogeneous groupings and classifies new data by rules generated from the splits.
- **Simple Matching Coefficient (SMC)** — a binary similarity measure computed as the proportion of exact 1-1 or 0-0 matches between two variables.
- **Income quintile** — classification of respondents into the poorest up to the richest 20 percent of households.

## Remember This

- Income quintile, saving behavior and gender lead in both the 2017 and 2021 rounds.
- Demographics predict resilience better than financial access, the paper concludes.
- No model exceeds 70 percent accuracy and the Findex resilience proxy is narrow.
- 2017 was face-to-face, 2021 phone-based; 980 and 992 observations were retained.
- Findex savings rose while the BSP survey's savings fell, an unresolved contradiction.
