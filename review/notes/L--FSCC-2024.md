# 2024 Financial Stability Report

`L--FSCC-2024` — Financial Stability Coordination Council (2024), *Financial Stability Coordination Council, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas* \[null]

## Summary

The FSCC's 2024 Financial Stability Report finds the Philippine financial system resilient but facing moderate risks, and highlights household-sector pressures: rising consumer debt, thinning savings buffers, and expenditures that grew nearly as fast as incomes, leaving many households vulnerable to income disruptions.

## Problem and Motivation

The report assesses systemic risks to the Philippine financial system and the macroprudential measures that safeguard it. Households face moderating inflation and lower unemployment but persistent pressure on debt repayment and cash buffers, and rising consumer debt raises concerns about debt-servicing capacity.

## Method

**Design.** The report states no formal design label; it is a descriptive and analytical macro-financial stability report that compiles and interprets official statistics from the BSP, PSA, and other agencies.
**Sample.** Not applicable (institutional report; it draws on official statistics such as the Consumer Expectations Survey randomized sample of approximately 5,000 households and the 2023 Family Income and Expenditure Survey, but defines no analytic sample of its own).
**Context.** geography: Philippines (national), with external and ASEAN comparisons; population: the Philippine financial system, corporates, and households; setting: national systemic-risk surveillance conducted by the FSCC, no fieldwork or human participants.

- Chapter 1 assesses global and domestic risks using a Systemic Risk Assessment Matrix.
- Chapter 2 examines banks, non-banks, and capital markets.
- Chapter 3, the household-relevant chapter, covers corporates and households; Sect. 3.2 reports consumer sentiment, household employment and income, expenditures versus incomes (2023 FIES), financial buffers and debt, and remittances.
- Chapter 4 sets out macroprudential and crisis-preparedness measures, including Box Article 3 on the countercyclical capital buffer.
- The report draws on the Q4 2024 CES (fielded 4-16 October 2024), the 2023 FIES, and BSP loan data.

## Key Findings

- num: Consumer sentiment was less pessimistic at -11.1 percent in Q4 2024 compared to -19.0 percent in Q4 2023 and -15.6 percent in Q3 2024 (Sect. 3.2, p. 45).
- num: Household consumption contributed approximately 3.7 percentage points to the overall 5.2 percent GDP growth in Q3 2024, but first nine months' growth was slower at 4.8 percent versus 5.7 percent in 2023 (Fig. 3.8, p. 45).
- num: Inflation averaged 3.2 percent in 2024 versus 6.0 percent in 2023; unemployment fell to 3.2 percent as of November 2024 from 3.6 percent a year earlier (p. 45; Table 3.2, p. 46).
- num: Average household incomes increased 15.0 percent while expenditures grew 12.8 percent (2023 versus 2021 FIES); for the bottom 30 percent, expenditure growth was 19.9 percent against 20.7 percent income growth (p. 47).
- num: Only 28.7 percent of all households were able to save in Q4 2024, a sharp drop from 41.8 percent in Q1 2020; savers were 16.7 percent of low-income and 40.6 percent of high-income households (p. 47).
- num: Household gross savings were one percent of total income in 2023, up from negative levels in 2020 to 2022 (p. 47).
- num: One in every four households availed loans in the last 12 months, while 13.7 percent intended to apply for a loan (p. 47).
- num: Outstanding household consumption loans reached ₱2.0 trillion in September 2024, up 22.8 percent from ₱1.6 trillion a year earlier; credit card loans grew 27.8 percent to ₱845.9 billion, or 42.2 percent of total household loans (Fig. 3.11, p. 48).
- num: Households using loan proceeds to purchase basic goods rose to 57.3 percent in Q4 2024 from 35.4 percent in Q1 2020 (Table 3.3, p. 48).
- num: Personal remittances reached US$34.6 billion from January to November 2024, of which US$31.1 billion were cash remittances (Fig. 3.12, p. 49).
- num: Non-performing consumer loans rose 9.9 percent YoY to ₱165.4 billion as of September 2024 (Fig. 3.13, p. 50).

## Limitations and Gaps

- The report acknowledges that the reported consumer loans cover only those from the banking sector, so the growing number of unsecured and unmonitored loans from online lending platforms is outside the measured household debt (p. 50).
- [unacknowledged] The household section blends sources with different reference periods and methodologies (Q4 2024 CES, 2023 FIES, September 2024 BSP loan data), so the figures are not strictly comparable across the narrative.
- [unacknowledged] The report is descriptive and reports point estimates without confidence intervals or significance tests, so no inference about precision is possible.
- The report itself flags persistent data gaps in systemic-risk surveillance and calls for more granular data for early detection of vulnerabilities (p. 54).

## Definitions

- **Consumer Expectations Survey (CES)** — a quarterly BSP survey conducted on a randomized sample of approximately 5,000 households across the Philippines (footnote 62, p. 44).
- **Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES)** — the Philippine Statistics Authority survey of household income and expenditure, conducted triennially and, from the 2023 round, every two years (footnote 70, p. 47).
- **Household financial buffers** — cash savings households can draw on to absorb unexpected income disruptions and unanticipated expenses (p. 47).

## Remember This

- FSCC 2024: Philippine financial system resilient but with moderate risks; household sector under debt and buffer pressure.
- Only 28.7 percent of households could save in Q4 2024, down from 41.8 percent in Q1 2020.
- Household expenditures grew 12.8 percent versus 15.0 percent income growth (FIES 2021 vs 2023).
- Household consumption loans hit ₱2.0 trillion in September 2024, up 22.8 percent year on year.
- Remittances reached US$34.6 billion in January to November 2024, a key household buffer.
