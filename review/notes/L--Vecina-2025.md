# Improving Financial Performance through Financial Literacy, Good Financial Practice and Fintech Adoption

`L--Vecina-2025` — Vecina (2025), *Divine Word International Journal of Management and Humanities, 4(2), 1688-1707*

## Summary

A descriptive-correlational survey of 90 Vigan City MSMEs finds high financial literacy, management practice and fintech adoption levels, each significantly correlated with financial performance, with management practice strongest (r = 0.684).

## Problem and Motivation

Micro, small and medium enterprises in the Philippines face financing constraints, weak financial records and limited planning discipline, so owners rely on intuition rather than structured financial decisions. Prior work treats financial literacy, management practice and fintech adoption separately, and this study examines whether the three combined explain MSME financial performance in Vigan City specifically.

## Method

**Design.** Descriptive-correlational research design (the paper's own label, "Research design", p. 1693): a descriptive survey of current variable levels paired with simple correlational analysis of relationships among them.
**Sample.** n = 90 MSME owners and managers in Vigan City, Ilocos Sur, Philippines, selected by total enumeration and drawn from sectors including hotels, souvenir shops, restaurants and retail.
**Context.** geography: Philippines — Vigan City, Ilocos Sur; population: MSME owners, managers and key decision-makers involved in financial management and decision-making; setting: face-to-face structured survey questionnaire distributed by the researcher to participating MSMEs.

- Adopt a descriptive-correlational design: the descriptive component presents the current level of each variable by survey, the correlational component examines relationships among them using simple correlational analysis.
- Set the population to MSMEs in Vigan City, with respondents being owners, managers or key decision-makers of MSMEs directly involved in financial management and decision-making, across sectors such as hotels and souvenir shops.
- Use total enumeration to include all 90 MSMEs in the study as respondents.
- Secure endorsement from the Graduate School program head to facilitate survey distribution; conduct a pilot test and obtain signed informed consent before participation.
- Build a researcher-made questionnaire covering financial literacy, financial management practices, fintech adoption and financial performance; validate it with experts and establish internal reliability through pilot testing.
- Analyse data with mean ratings on a five-point norm scale (Strongly Agree 4 to Strongly Disagree 1) and composite descriptive rating bands from Very High (3.26–4.00) down to Very Low (1.00–1.75).
- Apply simple correlational analysis (Pearson's r) to test whether each independent variable relates to financial performance.
- Test three hypotheses, H1–H3, each stating that one variable positively influences MSME financial performance.

## Key Findings

- num: The questionnaire instrument reached an expert validity rating with a mean of 4.25 and an excellent internal reliability of Cronbach's alpha = 0.973.
- num: 90 MSME owners and managers in Vigan City completed the structured survey.
- num: Financial literacy recorded a composite mean rating of 3.22, interpreted as High, with item means from 2.94 to 3.38.
- num: Financial management practices recorded a composite mean rating of 3.05, interpreted as High, with item means from 2.81 to 3.23.
- num: Financial technology adoption recorded a composite mean rating of 2.97, interpreted as High, with item means from 2.77 to 3.14.
- num: Financial performance recorded an overall mean rating of 2.94, interpreted as High, with item means from 2.69 to 3.09.
- num: Financial management practices had the strongest relationship with financial performance at r = 0.684 (p < .001), followed by financial technology adoption at r = 0.533 (p < .001) and financial literacy at r = 0.511 (p < .001).
- num: The overall correlation of the three independent variables with financial performance was r = 0.680 at a 0.01 significance level (two-tailed).
- num: All three hypotheses were supported, indicating that financial literacy, financial management practices and fintech adoption each positively influence MSME financial performance.
- num: The lowest-rated fintech item, using financial management tools to streamline operations, suggests limited use of more advanced features such as planning and forecasting tools.

## Limitations and Gaps

- [unacknowledged] The design is a single-wave, cross-sectional, self-report correlational survey, yet the hypotheses and discussion state that the variables "positively influence" and "significantly improve" financial performance. Nothing in the design supports a causal reading, and the paper never frames the results as associations only.
- [unacknowledged] Sample size and coverage are limited to 90 MSMEs in a single city (Vigan City, Ilocos Sur) selected by total enumeration, so the results cannot be generalised to other Philippine localities or to MSMEs outside the city's tourism-oriented business mix.
- [unacknowledged] All four constructs are measured by the same respondent's self-report on one instrument at one time, with no objective financial records, no behavioural log, no comparison group and no longitudinal follow-up, so common-method variance and reverse causality (better-performing firms being more able to finance literacy and app use) are unaddressed.
- [unacknowledged] No control variables are entered. Sector, firm size, age, owner education and years in business are all plausible confounders of both fintech adoption and financial performance, and none is reported even descriptively.
- [unacknowledged] The paper reports a single Pearson correlation per predictor with no multiple-regression estimates, no variance decomposition, no interaction tests, no effect-size interpretation and no confidence intervals, so the "predictors" framing in the title is not supported by the reported analysis.
- [unacknowledged] The composite descriptive rating bands and the item-level verbal descriptors printed in the results tables classify the same range of item means differently, so an item labelled with the strongest verbal category falls inside a band the same table calls the second-highest; the two schemes are never reconciled.
- Table numbering is inconsistent: financial technology adoption is printed under a repeated Table 1 caption (p. 1697) after Table 2 (p. 1696), and the next table is Table 4 (p. 1698), so no Table 3 appears in the article.
- Running headers throughout the article read "Abun et al." although the named authors are Vecina and Encarnacion, and the abstract cites only "Vecina and Encarnacion (2025)" with no co-author beyond the two.
- [unacknowledged] The Discussion attributes MSME underuse of financial planning technologies to "limited awareness or technical expertise" without measuring either construct, so that explanation is asserted rather than evidenced.
- [unacknowledged] The article reports no ethical approval identifier, funding source or conflict-of-interest statement, and the instrument's full item wording is only partially recoverable from the results tables.

## Definitions

- **Financial literacy** — Measured over ten indicators: interest rates, various financial products, financial concepts, diversification, budgeting and financial planning, evaluating loan terms, investment knowledge, setting financial goals, assessing risk tolerance, and planning for long-term financial needs.
- **Financial management practices** — Budgeting, financial control and strategic resource allocation, measured across ten indicators from creating a monthly budget to repaying debts in full and in time.
- **Financial technology adoption** — Use of mobile banking apps, financial management apps, digital payment platforms, online payment solutions for receiving customer payments, financial software for bookkeeping and planning, security and privacy confidence, smart devices, multi-device access, and financial management tools.
- **Financial performance** — Ten self-reported outcome indicators including revenue growth, meeting financial obligations, cash-flow sufficiency, return on investment, cost control, debt management, sales growth, market share, capital structure and risk management.
- **Overall DR (descriptive rating)** — The composite interpretation bands applied to composite means: Very High (3.26–4.00), High (2.51–3.25), Low (1.76–2.50) and Very Low (1.00–1.75).

## Remember This

- Vigan City MSME survey with n = 90, correlational design only.
- Composite means: literacy 3.22, practices 3.05, adoption 2.97, performance 2.94.
- Performance correlations: practices r = 0.684, adoption r = 0.533, literacy r = 0.511.
- Causal language is not supported by the cross-sectional design.
- Table numbering repeats Table 1 and skips Table 3.
